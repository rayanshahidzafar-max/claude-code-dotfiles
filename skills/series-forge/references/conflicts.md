# Conflict Rulings for Series Work

These rulings sit on top of `writers-room`'s fifteen, and on a series job they win wherever the two disagree. When a new conflict turns up, the skill closest to the deliverable wins. Record the ruling in the ledger.

| # | Topic | Skills that disagree | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Cliffhangers** | `sequel-craft` and `series-doctor` (soft cliffhangers, read-through hooks, hard ones with caution) vs. `story-design` (the climax must be absolute and irreversible) | Every installment answers its own main question with a real climax. The pull forward comes from a new question opened after it: a soft cliffhanger or a read-through hook. Hard cliffhangers only at chapter ends, or when the next book is already drafted. Never at the end of book one, and never at the end of a series. |
| S2 | **Recaps** | `sequel-craft` (recap options) vs. `reader-brain` and `story-forge` (need-to-know, no info dumps) | Woven reminders by default, each doing double duty (where we are, how much time has passed, what the last book cost). A front-matter recap only after a long gap or with a big cast. Fresh wording every time, checked by `sentence-craft`. |
| S3 | **Vague or specific** | `series-doctor` and `sequel-craft` (generalities for ages, dates, durations) vs. `style-audit` and `sentence-craft` (definite, specific, concrete) | Specific in the scene; flexible in the canon. Be exact about what's in the room, and loose on facts a later book may need to move. Use eras, not years, for distant history. |
| S4 | **Plan or discover** | `series-engine` and `sequel-craft` (plan in order to plant) vs. `story-forge` (door-closed discovery) | Plan the load-bearing plants, anything a later reveal depends on, and discover the rest in the draft. Don't publish an installment until the next one is far enough along to show what must be planted. |
| S5 | **Theme** | `series-engine` (name the series' argument up front) vs. `story-forge` and `story-design` (meaning found in the draft) | The series' thematic argument is part of the engine's design and lives in the bible. Each book's own angle is found in its second draft. Neither is ever stated in the story. |
| S6 | **Last lines** | `storytelling` (end on the strongest line; no "follow for more") vs. `sequel-craft` (the final page sells the next book) | Inside the story, the read-through hook is story: a new question, never an advertisement. The explicit "the next book is…" goes in the back matter, not in the last line. In social scripts, `storytelling` rules as usual. |
| S7 | **Spoilers** | `viral-hooks` (maximum intrigue) vs. `series-doctor` (every book is someone's way in) | Hooks, blurbs and recaps tease questions, never an earlier book's answers. A twist from an earlier book stays out of anything a newcomer might see first. |
| S8 | **Prequel suspense** | `story-design` (the major dramatic question: how will it turn out?) vs. `sequel-craft` (readers already know how it turns out) | In a prequel the dramatic question is how and why. Build dread and dramatic irony, and save true surprises for what the main books never revealed. |
| S9 | **Canon vs. a better idea** | `story-forge` (the story is the boss) vs. `series-engine` (the bible is canon) | A better idea may change the canon only if every published book still holds. Unpublished books can be revised to fit. A contradiction of a published book needs an explanation on the page, or the idea waits for a reboot. |
| S10 | **Honesty about the series** | launch copy (`sell-like-crazy`, `100m-offers`) vs. `series-doctor` (finish what you start) | Never announce a length that isn't written ("a twelve-book epic"). "Final book" means final. No fake scarcity on bundles or reader gifts, and never invent reader quotes. |
