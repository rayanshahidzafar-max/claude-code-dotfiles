# Negative Space, Point of View and Escalation

## Negative space

- Aim for deep unsettlement, not terror or disgust. Think of a dream where a familiar room is also somewhere else, and a woman who might be your mother or your teacher has no face. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- Build it from liminality, ambiguity, withheld information and subverted expectations, until the reader can't say what's wrong. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- Film shows the trick. Too much empty room around a subject makes us wait for something to step into frame. Prose can steer the eye the same way, toward dark corners and what's out of shot, and it pays here to make the reader work. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- Interest comes from what you hold back. Give only what the reader can't follow without, and make them want each fact before it arrives. [McKee ch15]
- False mystery is different. Hiding a fact the viewpoint character plainly knows, only to fake curiosity, abuses the reader. [McKee ch16]
- White space between scenes can carry what the text won't say. Set two passages side by side and let the reader draw the line between them. [McPhee, Structure]

## The home

- Strip a place of what makes it familiar and withhold the comforts of home. When a sunlit hallway goes black behind a shut door, the character is blinded, and the description goes with the light. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- The home turns threatening mostly through what the reader isn't allowed to see. If you aren't safe at home, where are you? [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]

## Point of view

- First person gives truthfulness and immediacy, and you control what the narrator reports: a retelling, a confession, a witness statement. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- An unreliable narrator takes away the reader's footing. One creeping realization about the narrator makes everything said before doubtful, and sends the reader back to the start. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- Second person is intimate and accusing, and it puts the reader inside. Let an anonymous watcher close in on the "you" before revealing the "I", and the threat jumps. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- A child narrator's naivety means full understanding is neither needed nor wanted. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- A bystander's reactions can raise the hair better than the victim's. [Highsmith ch7]

## Tense and title

- Present tense gives immediacy, because nobody knows what's coming. Past tense gives settled inevitability. A sudden flash-forward turns the reader from companion into a watcher waiting for the character's fate. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- A title plants an assumption. Use it, then overturn it at the end without explaining. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]

## Figures and sentences

- Set images side by side and let the reader connect them: a tender gesture, then bruises on the same hand. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]
- A sentence that seems to hold a thought still forming at the edge of consciousness invites re-reading, the way an unreadable face does. [Uncanny, Royle]
- What you notice matters because you noticed it. Get the words close to the thing itself before you give it a meaning. [Klinkenborg]
- Every figure must be graspable at reading speed. A metaphor the reader has to decode breaks the spell. [Cron ch6]
- Use definite, specific, concrete language. The slightly wrong detail only works if the right details around it are exact. [S&W R16]
- Don't overwrite. Ornate prose is hard to digest, so reread it later and cut the excess. [S&W V]

## Escalation

- Subvert the cliché at once: the car doesn't run dry on a lonely lane but among prosperous, gated, silent houses. A small odd event, and a reaction out of proportion to it, foreshadow what follows. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- Wrong-foot the Gothic template: not a shadowy figure but a floodlight and a cheerful young man in a white jacket. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- Give a full page of slightly odd, very specific detail before the turn, so the turn lands harder. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- Escalate by degrees, each a step past the last: too much food, then food too rich, then more when the character is already full, then someone insisting that he finish. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- The throwaway turn: something no rational account survives, shown without comment, while the character neither reacts nor asks. That is where plausibility ends. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- Dream logic, not randomness. Each strange image is there for a reason the reader sees on reflection, and the veils come off one at a time. Almost nothing happens, yet the character's place in the universe is shattered. [Uncanny, Dyson]
- Hold an ordinary detail a beat longer than usual and it stretches and distorts: a neighbour washing a clean car for an hour, a swing still moving on a windless day. [Uncanny, Shearman]
- True surprise splits expectation from result and is followed by a rush of understanding. A jolt with no insight behind it is the cheap kind. [McKee ch16]
- Foreshadow anything out of character, so the reader feels recognition rather than disbelief when it comes. [Cron ch11]
