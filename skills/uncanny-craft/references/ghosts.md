# Ghosts and Haunted Houses

## Seeing and not seeing

- A visible ghost's details do two jobs: they let us see it, and they tell its life. A prop can carry plot, and a scar can carry a whole history. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- A ghost is there and not there, and its presence advertises an absence. Nod to the form's clichés without obeying them. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- Work in traces: mist on a mirror, a door that won't stay shut, footsteps, wet prints. Traces allow doubt, from damp or a faulty router to guilt and paranoia. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- A poltergeist makes the home hostile. Let the ghost's actions mirror a real emotional pattern, like a controlling husband still slamming doors after his death. [Uncanny, Ashworth]

## Give the ghost a job

- Treat a ghost like any character: a purpose, a motive, and a chain of cause and effect toward something that matters. Ask what makes it visible now and what changes because of it. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- Ghosts pivot plots, and success makes them redundant: they leave when the work is done. Their jobs include messenger, teacher, avenger, trauma replaying itself, and revealer of a crime or of unfinished business. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- Even a ghost's moves must be traceable. What it does follows from what it wants, and each act triggers the next. [Cron ch8]
- Make the supernatural pervade the story. A ghost that only starts the plot could be swapped for any other device. [Ligotti, Consuming Horror notes]
- Show what the ghost can do early, the way a dangerous character's nature should be shown before it matters. [King, On Writing]

## The ghost's point of view

- Writing as the ghost means asking what you know now, and what it is to know things you can't say, or to talk and go unheard. Ghost points of view are really about being a person. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- The ghost who doesn't know it's dead makes an epiphany story. The reader works it out first, and the ghost's deeper ignorance is of the life it wasted. [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- Ghosts break physical law, so they can break point-of-view rules. Then make new rules and teach them through action: can it feel cold, eat, be heard, remember? A ghost might remember in jump cuts rather than flashbacks, carrying the shape of trauma (out-of-time repetition, a wound that never heals). [Uncanny, Ashworth]
- Once a world's rule is set, it binds the writer. Fantasy makes one great leap from reality and then obeys strict probability. [McKee ch2]
- Keep the rules consistent across a series, and never break one for a plot point. [Rosett, Practicalities]

## Iteration and witness

- Ghost stories are about iteration: the horror of coming back, each pass pushing reality further off true while freezing it. A ghost is the opposite of growth. The surplus presence works the same way: a room holding one person too many, or an empty second bed that waits to be filled. [Uncanny, Ward]
- M. R. James's rules, paraphrased: aim for a pleasing terror; use your own era and ordinary people; fix the place and time with plain detail; and at the climax keep the machinery a little dark. [Uncanny, Ward]
- Ghosts live in the "might" (possibility, uncertainty, omission, suggestion, obsessive echo), and a convinced disbeliever is the ideal witness, because the reader travels with him into dreadful belief. Choose the narrator whose beliefs the story needs. [Uncanny, Ward]
- Repetition can build like music. Key words shift from movement to movement, and the presence answers with an echo of the characters' own nervous laughter. [Uncanny, Ward]
- Characters who know their genre can be funny, and the joke sharpens the dread. A found document with gaps, its original burned, frightens through its fragments. [Uncanny, Ward]
- Ghost stories are about our struggle with the past and our powerlessness against time, the hope of putting things right, and a stand for being human against the dark. [Uncanny, Ward]
- Bring a ghost's memories in only when the reader needs and wants them, and dramatize each as a scene with its own turn. [McKee ch15]

## The seven questions

- Ghosts are one step from the known. No bite or curse is needed; dying is enough. [Uncanny, Buchanan]
- Answer seven questions and you have a ghost story. Where will it haunt? Who was it alive (maybe not human)? Who invited it? How will it surprise? What is its purpose? What results does it get? How will it leave? [Uncanny, Buchanan]
- A haunting can reveal character. Under the ghost's pressure a man acts against his morals, and only at the end sees where it came from. A late reveal recolours the whole tale, and perhaps the truest horror is going on existing: the banished ghost may be grateful. [Uncanny, Buchanan]
- Ghosts exist only as stories told afterwards. A ghost is a story not yet told, and you lay it by understanding that story. [Uncanny, Roberts]
