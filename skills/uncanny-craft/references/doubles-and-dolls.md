# Doubles, Dolls and Coincidence

## Freud, briefly

- The uncanny is the frightening that leads back to the once well known: what was meant to stay hidden has come out. The repressed returns, and repetition makes the effect. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- Freud describes two kinds:
  1. coincidence that seems to confirm beliefs we thought we had outgrown (wishes that come true, things that come alive, a novel's events happening);
  2. a revived childhood complex.
  - The first works only if the story seems to stand on common reality. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- Jung found Freud reductive. Art drawn from the collective unconscious speaks with many voices, and the mythic uncanny is the richer vein. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- Dig out a word's old meaning and it bites again. [Uncanny, Jarvis]

## Dolls, automata and puppets

- An earlier theorist placed the uncanny in doubt over whether a figure is a person or an automaton. A perfect, soulless, clockwork figure repels everyone except the one who has fallen for it, and puppets keep a grace we lost by becoming conscious. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- A crowd that behaves like machinery (identical men, a head turning slowly and stopping short, questions left unanswered) is uncanny without one impossible event. [Uncanny, Royle]
- A puppet is an effigy of us that knows nothing. When it seems to come alive, perception splits. [Ligotti, Preface]
- Characters who discover they are puppets, or paper, are a horror device in themselves. [Ligotti, Characters]

## Doubles

- A double recalls every future we pictured and lost. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- At night a glass wall shows only the family's reflections, and the doubles are coming. [Uncanny, Royle]
- Distort the rational voice too. The sensible character who insists it's all in the hero's head can herself be cold and mechanical. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- External facts that escape psychology keep the hesitation alive: the salesman really is the old tormentor, and the doll does move. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- Useful devices: a dismissed tale confirmed by a real object, a thing kept wrapped in rags, a tale inside a dream inside a tale. [Uncanny, Jarvis]
- The genre's menace keeps moving inward, from the stranger to the people we must trust and finally to the self. [McKee ch4]

## Selves dissolving

- Let people vanish from their own stories: a nameless "she" gone before page one is over, whom nobody can remember, whose photograph looks like a hundred other girls. [Uncanny, Moore]
- An emotional absence can become a literal one: a mother's missing love becomes a run of motherless heroines. [Uncanny, Moore]
- Stage crises of identity: missing from your own story, not resembling yourself, taking the wrong grown-up's hand. A repeated insistence on happiness is itself a menace. [Uncanny, Moore]
- Use your own experience. Notes taken during something real, selected and arranged, can cohere into something sinister, and a horror image dropped between routines breaches a boundary. [Uncanny, Moore]
- The uncanny can feel uniquely true. It says what plain speech can't, and strong emotional disturbance has a natural home there. [Uncanny, Moore]

## Coincidence

- Bring coincidence in early and let it gain meaning. Never let it pop in, turn a scene and vanish, and never use it to resolve an ending. [McKee ch16]
- Every recurrence still needs a visible road. Keep the dots in the reader's view, and make sure the payoff is physically possible. [Cron ch10]
