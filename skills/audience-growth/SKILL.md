# Audience Growth

This skill grows an audience by testing many versions and backing the winners. It produces the **variation sheet** (`references/variation-sheet.md`):
- the hook point for the account or concept;
- 5 to 10 or more hook variations per concept, each changing one thing;
- the audience slices to test them on;
- the metric and the kill threshold;
- what to pivot to, and what to double down on.

For what makes the winners spread, use `contagious-content`. For the opening line craft, use `viral-hooks`. For the platform's native format, use `platform-native`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- How fast people share decides how fast an account grows organically; the job is to get others to share for you. [Kane, Intro]
- Personal, individual contact with fans turns them into advocates who build the audience with you. [Kane, Intro]
- Never buy fake followers; they don't share, buy or show up. [Kane, Intro]
- Generic advice like "be authentic" doesn't grow anything without a method. [Kane, Intro]
- The method is an emotional message plus testing many variations, failing fast and moving on. [Kane, Intro]
- Don't sink the whole budget or week into one piece; spread it across versions and let the audience pick. [Kane, Intro]
- The share is the metric. Views and likes are vanity. [Kane ch5]
- Growth compounds. Small gains, kept up, add up to large numbers. [Kane ch1]
- Every follower costs something, money or time. Decide what a follower is worth to you before you spend. [Kane ch1]
- High-tempo testing beats waiting for one big idea; the fastest learners grow fastest. [Ellis ch4]

## How to use this in practice

1. **Find the hook point:** what makes this person or brand stand out, in a phrase that would work as a magazine cover headline. → `references/hook-point.md` [Kane ch3]
2. **Form a hypothesis** about format, story or theme. → `references/testing.md` [Kane ch1]
3. **Write 5 to 10 or more variations per concept,** changing one element each (headline, first frame, thumbnail, length, emotion, style). → `references/variation-sheet.md` [Kane ch1]
4. **Pick the audience slices** with the targeting checklist, one interest per test. → `references/targeting.md` [Kane ch2]
5. **Decide the metric and the threshold:** shares, cost per share, follow-on views. Set a kill time. [Kane ch1]
6. **Run a small paid test or a soft organic post** and read results within hours. → `references/testing.md` [Kane ch1]
7. **Ask why it worked.** Without the why, you're not getting smarter. [Kane ch1]
8. **Pivot or invest:** kill the losers, duplicate the winners, test fundamentally different messages as well as tweaks. [Kane ch4]
9. **Make it shareable:** service first, a reason to send it to one friend, subtitles, hook in seconds. → `references/shareable.md` [Kane ch5]
10. **Grow through others:** alliances, collaborations, shoutouts, and the platform's own rules. → `references/alliances-and-platforms.md` [Kane ch6]

## Where the books disagree

- **Paid or organic?** Kane treats well-run paid reach as today's organic. Vaynerchuk agrees but warns never to pay behind weak creative. Berger shows organic word of mouth beating ads. Resolution: pay small to test, then boost only what already performs.
- **Test or trust your gut?** Kane tests everything yet admits a famous ad tested badly and worked. Sugarman tests small before going big. Resolution: test by default; when instinct and data disagree, run one more cheap test rather than overruling either.
- **Copy what worked for others?** Kane borrows formats but warns a copied format doesn't guarantee a hit. Sugarman says pattern a good ad, don't copy it. Resolution: borrow the structure, never the content, and test it as your own hypothesis.
- **Grow broad or narrow?** Kane starts broad then narrows; Schaefer says bigger dilutes connection. Resolution: use broad tests to find the niche, then grow the niche deep before widening.
