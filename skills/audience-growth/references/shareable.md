# Shareable Content and the Full Funnel

## Service first

- The less you sell, the more you sell. [Kane ch5]
- Run the full funnel: shareable value first, then engagement content, then retarget the engaged with the ask. [Kane ch5]
- Keep roughly four parts giving to one part asking. [Kane ch5]
- Start from service: ask what you're for, how this helps, what you'd say if it were your final piece, how to make it the strongest piece on the topic, and what your real motive for posting is. [Kane ch5]
- Answer "why should anyone care?" before posting. [Kane ch5]
- Relatable personal truth, told with some vulnerability, connects. [Kane ch5]
- Give fans something to give their friends: the laugh, the touch to the heart. [Kane ch5]
- Ride a trending topic with a brand twist. [Kane ch5]
- Cast people the audience relates to. [Kane ch5]
- Sometimes go with your gut; a famous ad tested poorly and became a hit. [Kane ch5]
- Make the right people laugh, not everyone. [Kane ch5]
- Unexpected outcomes at unexpected moments, an idol in a humble setting, a twist at the end. [Kane ch5]
- Lifestyle moments around the product can outperform the product itself. [Kane ch5]
- Don't tell people to tag friends; give a reason, like the friend who'd be most scared. [Kane ch5]
- Get to it fast: grab attention within about four seconds, no warm-up. [Kane ch5]
- High production values don't make a piece shareable. [Kane ch5]
- Keep one clear message. [Kane ch5]
- Subtitle videos; most are watched with the sound off. [Kane ch5]
- Each piece must stand on its own. [Kane ch5]
- Give, give, give, then ask. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Ask "would I share this?" before you post. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Content that hits at least two of social currency, triggers, emotion, public, practical value and stories spreads further. [Berger, Intro]
- A gentle jab, one mental step from the product, often works better than talking about the product. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Ride what the audience is already talking about and switch when they switch. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Reply to comments and tag people; the conversation is part of the content. [Vaynerchuk R9]
- Useful, narrow content gets forwarded to the one friend it fits. [Berger ch5]
- A high-arousal feeling makes people act; a low-arousal one makes them scroll on. [Berger ch3]
- Keep the brand inside the story so the retelling carries it. [Berger ch6]
- A referral reward should pay in the product's core value. [Ellis ch5]
- Built-in sharing that happens through normal use spreads further than a request to share. [Ellis ch5]
- Variable rewards keep people checking back for the next piece. [Eyal ch4]
- People share what they're proud to be part of; help members share outside the group. [Schaefer ch10]
- Write like one person talking to one person. [Sugarman ch15]
- Promise something curious and pay it off later in the piece. [Sugarman ch10]
- On image-led platforms an occasional ask in the caption is fine; too many calls to action reads as spam. [Vaynerchuk R6]
