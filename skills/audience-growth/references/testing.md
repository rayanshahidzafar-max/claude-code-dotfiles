# Hypothesize, Test, Pivot

## The loop

- Hypothesize about format, story or theme. [Kane ch1]
- Test with a low-cost proof before investing. [Kane ch1]
- Pivot: invest in the winner or form a new hypothesis. [Kane ch1]
- Borrow what's working for others as a starting hypothesis. [Kane ch1]
- Short-term tests inform this week's content; trends across months shape long-term strategy. [Kane ch1]
- Check every result against the brand. A winning format that's off-brand gets dropped. [Kane ch1]
- Learn why it worked. A thousand shares on one and one share on another is a lesson only if you find the reason. [Kane ch4]
- A tweak to one word or colour can change everything. [Kane ch4]
- Launch many variations at a time and see which ones the audience picks. [Kane ch4]
- Test fundamentally different messages as well as small tweaks. [Kane ch4]
- Think about where the viewer is in their knowledge of the brand; a stranger needs a different piece from a fan. [Kane ch4]
- Keep records over time so you can judge whether a complaint or a win is new. [Kane ch4]
- Most experiments fail; that's the point of running many. [Ellis ch4]
- Small teams should run high-impact tests first; tiny tweaks need huge samples to show a difference. [Ellis ch3]
- Score ideas by impact, confidence and ease, and don't argue the scores too long. [Ellis ch4]
- Write every test down with the result so the lessons outlive the test. [Ellis ch4]
- Test small before going big. [Sugarman ch10]
- Test the time, the slang, the tagline, the hashtag, the image. [Vaynerchuk R1]

## Paid amplification

- Hope is a bad strategy. Efficient paid distribution is the new organic. [Kane ch1]
- Ad platforms reward content people respond to with cheaper reach, so good creative lowers cost. [Kane ch1]
- Separate each interest into its own test so you know which one worked. [Kane ch1]
- Start broad, narrow later. [Kane ch1]
- If a piece doesn't land in the first hour, kill it. [Kane ch1]
- Set thresholds in advance, such as a maximum cost per share. [Kane ch1]
- Duplicate a winning ad set rather than raising its budget. [Kane ch1]
- Retarget the people who engaged, and build look-alike audiences from them. [Kane ch2]
- Boost only what already performs organically; the community is a free filter for weak creative. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Never pay to push weak creative. [Vaynerchuk R3]

## Listening

- Treat the audience as a partner and respond quickly. [Kane ch4]
- Search shows active interest (pull); social is push. Use search trends to size and pick topics. [Kane ch4]
- Listen to what people say about the topic, and dig into why they say it. [Kane ch4]
- Let the community act as editors of what goes out. [Kane ch4]
- Talk to users firsthand. [Kane ch4]
- Growth is a marathon, not a rocket ship. [Kane ch4]

## From the rest of the room

- A growth lead sets one focus area and a weekly tempo of tests. [Ellis ch1]
- Decide by data, not by company lore. [Ellis ch1]
- Start small: one product, one channel, one metric. [Ellis ch1]
- Clicks are a poor proxy; track what people do downstream. [Ellis ch2]
- Numbers say what happened, not why; interview people to find out. [Ellis ch3]
- A modest monthly improvement compounds to a large yearly gain. [Ellis ch4]
- The control wins a tie. [Ellis ch4]
- Double down on a winner; nearby variations often win too. [Ellis ch9]
- Run a minimum number of tests every week, even when growth is good, so it doesn't stall. [Ellis ch9]
- Boxers study film of their opponents; study the analytics. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Run several ideas on different platforms and take the strongest as the seed for a big spend. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Don't drop a campaign just because the team is bored with it; the audience is often just getting familiar with it. [Sugarman ch19]
- Timing changes results, so test when, not only what. [Sugarman ch19]
