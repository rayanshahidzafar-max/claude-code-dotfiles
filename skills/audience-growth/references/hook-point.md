# Hook Points

## Finding the hook point

- A hook point is the thing that makes you stand out in a crowded feed, in a few words. [Kane ch3]
- A bold specific promise beats a plain description: a book called a four-hour work week travels where "a guide to working fewer hours" wouldn't. [Kane ch3]
- Exercise: write the magazine cover headline that would make someone pick up an article about you or the product. [Kane ch3]
- Start from questions the audience already asks themselves, like what happens when you sleep. [Kane ch3]
- Specific, emotional and novel beats vague. A named person, a number, a twist. [Kane ch3]
- Test titles cheaply before investing in the full piece. [Kane ch3]
- The headline matters most; test it first. [Kane ch1]
- The hook point must match the content. Clickbait wins the click and loses the follower. [Kane ch1]
- Tie dry topics to what is already popular: inspiration and aspiration, news, entertainment, comedy, pets, a celebrity angle. [Kane ch3]
- Ask whether the piece will create an emotional response. If not, rework the hook. [Kane ch3]
- People share what makes them look good, so the hook point should give the sharer social currency. [Kane ch3]
- Stay unattached to any single segment or angle; let the tests decide. [Kane ch3]
- A message that tells people what it is in a few words, like a thousand songs in your pocket, lands fast. [Ellis ch5]
- Small word changes in a headline can swing results hugely. [Ellis ch5]
- One word change in copy can lift response. [Sugarman ch11]

## Speak to how people perceive

- Communication is about the receiver. Different people take in messages in different ways. [Kane ch3]
- Thinkers respond to logic and facts. [Kane ch3]
- Persisters respond to values and opinions. [Kane ch3]
- Harmonizers respond to feelings and care. [Kane ch3]
- Imaginers respond to reflection and quiet ideas. [Kane ch3]
- Rebels respond to humour and play. [Kane ch3]
- Promoters respond to action, charm and a challenge. [Kane ch3]
- Feelings, logic and humour together cover most of an audience; write variations that lean into each. [Kane ch3]

## Building many hooks from one piece

- One interviewer built each conversation around topics that each fan niche cared about, cut clips, made many variations of each, pushed each clip to its niche, then invited them to the full interview. [Kane ch3]
- Cut a long piece into 3 to 10 clips, each in a few formats (single image, slideshow, footage), for 10 to 100 variations. [Kane ch1]
- Quote images are easy to test because they have fewer variables than video. [Kane ch1]
