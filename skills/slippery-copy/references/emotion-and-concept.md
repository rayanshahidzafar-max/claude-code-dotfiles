# Emotion, Concept and Cure

## Emotion

- Every word carries emotion and a small story. [Sugarman ch11]
- Strong ads pour out feeling, then shape it with care. [Sugarman ch11]
- Sell on emotion, then justify with logic, the way luxury car buyers cite engineering. [Sugarman ch11]
- A warm promise of a prompt, courteous refund beats a cold list of terms. [Sugarman ch11]
- A scene of a frustrated person in a phone booth sold better than ads full of specifications. [Sugarman ch11]
- One word change, like a plainer verb, can lift response. [Sugarman ch11]
- Meaning creates emotion. For every claim, ask "so what?" until you reach what it means to the buyer's life. [Edwards S10]
- Tie the meaning to love, fear, pride, vanity, greed, longing or freedom. [Edwards S10]
- Without emotion you compete on price. [Edwards S10]
- Lead with a high-arousal feeling; it moves people to act. [Berger ch3]

## Sell the concept

- Sell the concept, not the product. People buy what it means. [Sugarman ch12]
- The exception: when the product itself is new, sell the product. [Sugarman ch12]
- A smoke detector sold as a nose on the ceiling, a familiar concept for something new. [Sugarman ch12]
- Combining products into one concept can sell both. [Sugarman ch12]
- Price changes the concept. A higher price can signal a serious product where a low one signals a toy. [Sugarman ch12]
- Find the inherent drama in the product. [Sugarman ch12]
- Write in the language and market that fits; a message that lands fast can reshape the product. [Ellis ch5]

## Cure, not prevention

- Sell the cure, not the prevention. People pay to fix a problem they have, not one they might have. [Sugarman ch20]
- Reposition a preventive product as a cure where you honestly can. [Sugarman ch20]
- Emphasise the curative parts of what it does. [Sugarman ch20]
- Belief matters: people must believe the cure works. [Sugarman ch20]
- Habit products start as vitamins and become painkillers; copy can name the itch. [Eyal ch1]
