# The Slippery Slide

## The first sentence

- Make the first sentence short, almost incomplete, so it's easy to start. [Sugarman ch5]
- The first sentence is the locomotive; it pulls the rest of the train. [Sugarman ch5]
- Don't open with the sales pitch. Benefits come later, once the reader is sliding. [Sugarman ch5]
- The headline and subhead together, around sixteen words, set up the first sentence. [Sugarman ch22]
- The second sentence must keep the momentum of the first. [Sugarman ch18]
- In video, the first spoken words are the headline. [Edwards S17]
- The first three seconds of a video ad decide whether it's watched. [Edwards S28]

## The buying environment

- Create the right environment for buying in the layout and the first paragraphs, the way a gallery leads a serious buyer to a quiet back room. [Sugarman ch6]
- A discount look and an upscale look set different expectations; match the look to the product. [Sugarman ch6]
- Word choice and integrity build the environment as much as design does. [Sugarman ch6]
- A clean, uncluttered layout signals integrity. [Sugarman ch19]

## Getting a yes

- Get the reader nodding yes with true, believable statements. [Sugarman ch7]
- One "no" or one moment of disbelief can lose them. [Sugarman ch7]
- Speak in the reader's own language. [Sugarman ch7]
- When stuck, ask a question the reader will answer yes to. [Edwards S28]

## Keeping them sliding

- Each element should force the reader to the next. [Sugarman ch8]
- Open with a short story or an odd news item tied to the product. [Sugarman ch8]
- Keep a file of odd articles for openings. [Sugarman ch8]
- Self-deprecating honesty, even taking the product apart, can pull readers in. [Sugarman ch8]
- Seeds of curiosity are brief closing lines on a paragraph that promise more. Don't overdo them. [Sugarman ch10]
- Promise a payoff and reveal it later. [Sugarman ch10]
- Personality and honesty show through copy. [Sugarman ch10]
- Paragraph headings break up long copy and give scanners a way in. [Sugarman ch18]
- Vary rhythm so the reader doesn't drift. [Sugarman ch18]
- Don't assume constraints that aren't there; the best idea is often the one you doubted. [Sugarman ch9]
- Each part's job is to move the reader to the next part, like a bucket brigade. [Edwards S17]
- Curiosity is the strongest driver of a click. [Edwards S28]
- The brain responds more to anticipating a reward than to getting it; an open question pulls. [Eyal ch4]
