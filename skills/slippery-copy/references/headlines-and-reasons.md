# Headlines, Reasons and Hooks (support)

Sugarman's slide starts at the headline. These templates from the rest of the room fill it.

## Reasons to buy

- No reason, no purchase. [Edwards S3]
- Reasons include making money, saving money, saving time, avoiding effort, escaping pain, comfort, health, praise, love and status. [Edwards S3]
- Tie several reasons to the product, like tying down a tarp at several corners. [Edwards S3]
- Force ten answers to each reason question; the real ones come after the obvious ones. [Edwards S3]
- Search the copy for I, me, my and we, and rewrite from the reader's side. [Edwards S4]

## Headlines

- Most people read the headline and few read on, so a great headline with average copy beats the reverse. [Edwards S6]
- A headline should stop the right people and repel the wrong ones. [Edwards S6]
- "How to get the result in a believable time, even if the usual excuse" removes an objection inside the headline. [Edwards S6]
- Odd numbers of ways seem more credible. [Edwards S6]
- Mistake and warning headlines work, but crying wolf wears them out. [Edwards S6]
- Spend real time on the headline, often half the time spent on the piece. [Edwards S6]
- Test new headlines against the winner. [Edwards S6]
- Specific, emotional and novel headlines beat vague ones. [Kane ch3]
- Write five to ten headline variations and let the audience pick. [Kane ch1]
- The hook must match the content, or the click becomes a lost follower. [Kane ch1]

## Hooks and stories

- A hook is an angle that creates curiosity; it differentiates the message, where a unique selling point differentiates the product. [Edwards S29]
- Hooks are often hidden stories the seller takes for granted, and they must be true. [Edwards S29]
- Stories make people thirsty; the copy tells them where to drink. [Edwards S20]
- Myths, misconceptions and mistakes make content that sells, and mistakes pull hardest. [Edwards S20]
- Give away the what and why; sell the how. [Edwards S20]
- Take a stand; people buy from personalities, and the middle earns nothing. [Edwards S21]
- Skimmers read the headline, subheads, bold text, pictures and P.S.; they should get the gist from those alone. [Edwards S31]
- A story with the brand at its centre is retold with the brand in it. [Berger ch6]
- Practical, specific value gets forwarded. [Berger ch5]
- Content counts as cool when sharing it says something about the sharer. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Polls and questions invite replies, and replies spread the post. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Make the brand read like a real person, not a company. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- A brand voice should be consistent, not signed by whichever staffer posted. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Write for the community's language and let members colour outside the lines. [Schaefer ch7]
- Telling people they're free to say no makes them more likely to say yes. [Eyal ch4]
