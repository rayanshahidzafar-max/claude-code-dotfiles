# Sequence, Length and Editing

## Know before you write

- Curiosity, broad interests and many experiences feed copy; new ideas are new combinations of old pieces. [Sugarman ch1]
- To break a block, take a few random words and force links to the problem. [Sugarman ch1]
- Learn the product until you know it better than anyone. Days of questions turned a dull watch feature into a memorable name. [Sugarman ch2]
- Know the customer, and be the customer if you can. [Sugarman ch2]
- Look for the overlooked feature everyone else missed. [Sugarman ch2]
- Know when the product is bought: insurance sells when a neighbour has a loss. [Sugarman ch2]
- Don't scare people with statistics. [Sugarman ch2]
- Study, list the points, then stop and do something pleasant; the subconscious works on it. [Sugarman ch13]
- Then pour the copy out without editing. [Sugarman ch13]
- Seven steps: become the expert, know the prospect, write the headline and first sentence, write freely, edit, incubate, take a final look. [Sugarman ch22]
- Write in chunks, not as one block, and get the draft done fast; editing is easier than writing. [Edwards S19]
- Keep a swipe file of copy that works. [Edwards S30]

## The copy sequence

- Anticipate the reader's next question and answer it, in the order they'd ask it. [Sugarman ch16]
- A typical flow: interest, how it's different, how it works, quality, why the price is fair, lasting value, service, the ask. [Sugarman ch16]
- Restate the problem several ways so every reader recognises it. [Sugarman ch16]
- Pattern a good ad's flow; don't copy its layout. [Sugarman ch16]
- Problem, agitate, solve works well for cold audiences. [Edwards S15]
- Before, after, bridge works for any temperature of audience. [Edwards S15]

## Length

- Run as long as it takes to get the action. [Sugarman ch14]
- People read everything about what they're intensely interested in. [Sugarman ch14]
- Higher prices and unusual products need more copy; catalog items, strong photos and low prices need less. [Sugarman ch14]
- Long copy on a cheap, simple product can seem suspicious. [Edwards S16]
- Write from one person to one person, with I and you and a name. [Sugarman ch15]
- A warm personal letter beats a form letter. [Sugarman ch15]

## Editing

- Use the fewest words that carry the same emotion. [Sugarman ch17]
- Cut needless "that" phrases, combine sentences and remove words that do no work. [Sugarman ch17]
- Reorder sentences for emotional effect. [Sugarman ch17]
- Rest it overnight, then look again. [Sugarman ch17]
- Have someone else proof it; errors cost credibility. [Sugarman ch17]
- Readability: aim around an eighth- to tenth-grade level, and open simplest. Don't obsess over the score. [Sugarman ch21]
