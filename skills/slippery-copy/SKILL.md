# Slippery Copy

This skill writes copy the reader slides through to the action. It works on a caption, an ad, an email, a product page or a long sales letter, and it produces the **copy sheet** (`references/copy-sheet.md`):
- the concept being sold, not just the product;
- the headline, the subhead and a short first sentence;
- the buying environment and the yes-statements;
- the sequence of questions the reader will ask, in order;
- the triggers used and the objection resolved;
- the call to action;
- an edit pass to the fewest words.

For the hook line itself, use `viral-hooks`. For platform format and the jab before the ask, use `platform-native`. For the reader's anti-AI sniff test, use `anti-ai-writing`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- Copy exists to get someone to trade money they worked for in return for what you sell. Everything else serves that. [Sugarman, Intro]
- Write the print version first, even for other media; it forces you to find the essence and the selling points. [Sugarman, Intro]
- Copywriting is a mental process. Start, practise, and expect a terrible first draft; polishing is where the work is. [Sugarman ch3]
- Every graphic element (headline, subhead, photo, caption, layout) exists to get the first sentence read. [Sugarman ch4]
- The first sentence exists to get the second read, and so on down the slide. [Sugarman ch5]
- Once someone has read about a quarter of the copy, they're likely to read it all. [Sugarman ch8]
- Feeling drives the purchase; reasons come afterwards to defend it. [Sugarman ch11]
- Sell the concept, not the product. [Sugarman ch12]
- Length is right when it gets the action, and anything past that is waste. [Sugarman ch14]
- Everyone hates being sold but loves to buy. [Kane ch11]

## Where the books disagree

- **Long or short?** Sugarman lets copy run long when interest is high; Vaynerchuk says long text sinks a social post. Resolution: the caption is short and slides to the click; the page behind the click can be as long as the price and complexity need.
- **Sell in the post or not?** Sugarman writes to sell; Vaynerchuk and Kane jab first and ask rarely. Resolution: on social, most posts give; the selling copy goes in the right hook and on the page.
- **Hope and health claims.** Sugarman's hope trigger leans on implied results. Resolution: never imply what you can't back up. Imply only what's true, and state it plainly.
- **Fear.** Sugarman uses fear carefully; Berger says anxiety spreads. Resolution: name a real risk once, then move to the cure.
