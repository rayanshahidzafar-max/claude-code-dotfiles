# Conflict Rulings

These skills were written separately. Where they disagree, the room rules as follows. When a new conflict turns up, the skill closest to the deliverable wins. Record the ruling in the ledger.

| # | Topic | Skills that disagree | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Em dashes** | `anti-ai-writing` (a formatting tell) vs. `style-audit` (Rule 8 allows a dash for an abrupt break) | **Written copy** (social, prose, persuasion): replace with periods, commas, colons or parentheses. **Fiction:** keep a dash only for an abrupt break or interruption, such as cut-off dialogue, where no simpler mark works. Never use one as a spare comma. **Spoken scripts:** doesn't apply. |
| 2 | **Metaphor and analogy** | `anti-ai-writing` (five tests, banned families) vs. `sentence-craft` / `style-audit` (sparing, exact metaphor) | **Social, business and prose copy:** `anti-ai-writing`'s tests. **Fiction:** `sentence-craft`'s test (a sudden, exact renaming, used rarely) plus `style-audit` (sparingly, never mixed). Craft terms in notes, such as McPhee's "iceberg," aren't prose. |
| 3 | **Hook vs. lead** | `viral-hooks` vs. `reader-brain`, `sentence-craft`, `revision-passes` | **SOCIAL, PERSUADE:** `viral-hooks` decides (its four killers and the platform deadline). **STORY, PROSE:** `viral-hooks` proposes; `reader-brain`'s first-page test and `sentence-craft`'s audition decide. An opening that promises more than the piece delivers is vetoed everywhere. |
| 4 | **"You/your" framing** | `viral-hooks` vs. fiction point of view | **Social hooks:** "you" framing, or the I-framed story patterns that `viral-hooks` and `storytelling` sanction. **Fiction:** never; relevance comes from the protagonist's stakes. |
| 5 | **Real vs. invented** | `storytelling` ("never invent") and every persuasion skill vs. `story-forge` | **Anything presented as true:** real material only. **Fiction:** invention is the craft. Label fiction as fiction when posting it. |
| 6 | **Reading level** | `dumbify` vs. `sentence-craft` and literary voice | **Social, teaching, selling:** `dumbify`'s targets. **Fiction and literary essays:** `dumbify` only flags sentences a reader must read twice. It never flattens rhythm (its own rule) or the narrator's diction. |
| 7 | **"Not X, it's Y" contrast** | `anti-ai-writing` (banned when hollow) vs. `viral-hooks` (contrast drives curiosity) | `anti-ai-writing`'s hollow-vs-earned test applies everywhere: keep a contrast only when B is concrete and the piece delivers it. Characters may speak in contrasts when B names something real. |
| 8 | **Grammar vs. casual voice** | `style-audit` vs. social voice | **Social and spoken:** usage errors only. **Formal prose and fiction narration:** the full audit. Record deliberate fragments as kept, not fixed. |
| 9 | **Last word** | `style-audit` vs. `anti-ai-writing` | Order is `style-audit`, then `anti-ai-writing`. If an `anti-ai-writing` edit breaks usage in formal prose, restore correctness. Otherwise its priority order decides: accurate > clear > specific > voiced > stylish. |
| 10 | **Length** | `storytelling` (runtime budget) vs. `story-forge` / `revision-passes` (cut 10%) | **Scripts:** the runtime budget comes first (about 2 words a second). Cut beats, never the lens or the last line. **Written pieces:** cut about 10% after the draft works. |
| 11 | **Transitions** | `anti-ai-writing` (dead transitions), `sentence-craft` (forced logic words), `storytelling` (but/therefore) | All agree: cut "moreover," "furthermore" and "additionally." Between beats, the logic is "but" or "therefore." Say it aloud in spoken pieces; let the order carry it in written narrative. |
| 12 | **Endings and CTAs** | `storytelling` (last dab; no "follow for more"), `anti-ai-writing` (cut an ending that repeats the point) vs. `storybrand` and `sell-like-crazy` (clear call to action) | End on the strongest line; never recap. **Non-selling social:** no CTA in the script's last line; put it in the caption if needed. **PERSUADE:** the CTA is required, explicit, and placed right after the last line and in the caption. |
| 13 | **Stating the point** | `reader-brain` / `story-design` (never state the theme) vs. `expert-secrets-playbook` (state the one belief) | Different jobs. Fiction never states its point; persuasion states its one belief plainly. |
| 14 | **How many options** | `viral-hooks` (3 variants) vs. `sentence-craft` (audition 10–15 first sentences) | **Social:** 3 labeled variants unless the user asks otherwise. **Fiction first lines:** generate 10–15, show the top 3, and mark the chosen one. |
| 15 | **Honesty** | the persuasion skills' ethical limits | Enforced in every lane: no fake scarcity, fake urgency, invented testimonials, or guarantees nobody will honor. |
| 16 | **Series jobs** | `series-forge`'s rulings vs. the rulings above | On any sequel, prequel, continuation, spin-off or series plan, `series-forge/references/conflicts.md` applies on top of this table and wins where the two disagree. |
