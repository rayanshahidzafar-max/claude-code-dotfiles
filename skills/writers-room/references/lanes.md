# Lanes: each skill's exact job

Every lane lists its skills in firing order, with the one job each does here. The always-on core (`viral-hooks`, `style-audit`, `anti-ai-writing`) appears in every lane at the point where it works best.

## STORY — stories, chapters, scenes, fan fiction

1. **`story-forge`** runs the stations and keeps the story folder: brief, world, design, reader check, draft, reread, second draft, revision log, final. Everything below plugs into one of its stations.
2. **`worldbuilding`** (station 1). Build only as deep as the story needs. Skip it for a real-world setting that needs no invented causes.
3. **`story-design`** (station 2). Produces the design sheet and step-outline.
   - **The genre add-on** runs first when the job is tagged (see GENRE ADD-ONS below). Its sheet goes at the top of `02-design.md`, and the step-outline is built to honour it.
   - **`storytelling`** joins here. Write "but" or "therefore" between every pair of scenes in the step-outline. If only "and then" fits, a causal link is missing, so fix the design. Also write the target for the final line (its "last dab") beside the climax.
4. **`reader-brain`** (station 3). A reader report on the design. If there's a genre sheet, the report checks the design against it.
5. **Draft** (station 4), using `story-forge`'s drafting craft.
   - **`viral-hooks`** joins here. Generate 10–15 candidate first sentences with its levers. Its four hook killers carry over to fiction:
     - DELAY: throat-clearing before the story starts.
     - CONFUSION: a first sentence that needs a reread.
     - IRRELEVANCE: nobody to care about and nothing at stake.
     - DISINTEREST: no sign that all is not as it seems.

     Hand the batch to `sentence-craft`'s first-sentence audition. `reader-brain`'s first-page test picks the winner. No bait, and no "you."
6. **`revision-passes`** (station 7). Runs the passes and the 10% cut.
   - **The genre add-on** returns with its own revision checks (see GENRE ADD-ONS below).
   - **`dumbify`** joins here as a clarity check only. It flags any sentence a reader must read twice, then splits it or fixes the syntax. It never lowers the register or strips the voice.
7. **`sentence-craft`** (station 8).
8. **`style-audit`** (station 9), full audit.
   - **`title-craft`** joins here for the final title. The working title is replaced, and the title sheet goes in the revision log.
9. **`anti-ai-writing`**. The final pass catches vocabulary tells, hollow reframes, significance inflation and flat rhythm. Handle em dashes and metaphors by the rulings in `conflicts.md`.
   - **`storytelling`** returns for one test: is the last line strong enough to be quoted alone?
10. **On request, or in FULL STACK:** the SOCIAL pack and the VIDEO lane, built from the finished story.

## SERIES — sequels, prequels, continuations, spin-offs, series plans

1. **`series-forge`** takes the job. It finds the canon (reading earlier installments in full), opens the series folder (`stories/series/<slug>/`), and names the mode: SEQUEL, PREQUEL, CONTINUATION, SPIN-OFF or SERIES PLAN.
2. **`series-doctor`**, **`series-engine`** and **`sequel-craft`** run in the mode's order. Between them they produce the checkup, the series outline and bible, the installment plan, the loop ledger and the reader path.
   - **`title-craft`** sets the series name and the book-title pattern at the plan stage, early, so covers and titles stay in step.
3. **The STORY lane** runs on the installment, with the installment plan as the SERIES block of `00-brief.md`. Each station gets a series duty: world facts checked against the bible, a reader check as newcomer and as returning reader, a continuity pass, fresh wording for recaps, and the last page tested as a hook.
4. **The launch pack** (on by default in `series-forge`): series line and blurb (titles checked against the pattern by `title-craft`), back-matter teaser and reading order, social pack, Seedance shotlist, and a reader gift or offer only when there's something to give or sell.
5. **`style-audit`**, then **`anti-ai-writing`**, as always.

## GENRE ADD-ONS — suspense, mystery, horror, the uncanny

These aren't lanes of their own. They ride on STORY and SERIES jobs, and on fiction inside SOCIAL, when the request or the brief names the genre. Each one writes a sheet for `02-design.md` and comes back at revision.

| Add-on | Fires for | Sheet (station 2) | Revision checks (station 7) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `suspense-craft` | suspense, thriller, crime, mystery, whodunit, detective, police, heist, noir, psychological or domestic suspense | SUSPENSE SHEET, plus a plausibility log for every fact the plot turns on | danger felt on every page; the plausibility log complete; in a mystery, clues fair and the solution findable; no slack middle |
| `cosmic-horror` | cosmic, eldritch or Lovecraftian horror, weird or existential horror, forbidden knowledge, possession, puppets | DREAD SHEET | the thing never named or explained; the lead's defenses stripped in order; no back door at the end; vague horror adjectives cut, febrile figures kept concrete |
| `uncanny-craft` | the uncanny, weird and eerie, ghost stories, haunted houses, folk horror, Gothic, doubles and dolls, dark fairy tales | UNCANNY SHEET | the strange kept dark, never the cause and effect; the escalation builds; the human outcome closed and the strange left open |

1. **Station 2.** The add-on runs before `story-design`. Its sheet goes at the top of `02-design.md`, and `story-design` builds the step-outline to honour it.
2. **Station 3.** `reader-brain` checks the design against the sheet. Is the danger felt (suspense)? Is the dread anchored from page one (cosmic horror)? Is the familiar thing set up before it turns (uncanny)?
3. **Station 4.** The draft follows the sheet's prose notes. The first-line candidates must suit the genre's opening: movement for suspense, an atmosphere already anchored in death for cosmic horror, the ordinary made slightly wrong for the uncanny.
4. **Station 7.** The add-on returns with the revision checks in the table.
5. **Two tags.** A ghost story that's also a murder mystery gets both sheets. Where they clash, the genre the user named first leads, and the other lends its tools.
6. **SERIES jobs.** The add-on runs on the installment, and its rules go into the series bible (the strange element's rules, the recurring investigator's methods), so later books keep them.

## WORLD — settings, factions, histories, lore bibles, game worlds

1. **`worldbuilding`**. The dossier, with pressure points.
2. **`story-design`**. Turns each pressure point into a story question: who wants what, and what the world does to stop them.
3. **`reader-brain`**. Cuts anything that doesn't bear on a character's struggle. The exception is a deliberate reference bible, where completeness is the job.
4. **`sentence-craft`**. For any lore text meant to be read.
5. **`dumbify`**. For reader- or player-facing entries (wikis, codex entries, onboarding lore). Aim for the 8th-grade body target and keep invented names intact.
6. **`viral-hooks`**. Entry titles and first lines.
7. **`style-audit`**, then **`anti-ai-writing`**.

## SOCIAL — hooks, reels, captions, carousels, threads, posts, newsletters

0. **Setup:** load the client or person operating skill if one applies, and find a voice sample (see SKILL.md).
1. **`story-design`** + **`reader-brain`**. A two-minute pass for narrative pieces:
   - whose story it is;
   - what they wanted;
   - what changed;
   - the gap between what they expected and what happened.

   That gap is usually the "but" the piece hangs on. Skip this pass for pure tips and listicles.
2. **`storytelling`**. Covers the lens, the first and last lines, the but/therefore beats, rhythm, tone and the length budget (about 2 spoken words per second). It drives narrative pieces. For tutorials and listicles, use only its Dance and Rhythm.
3. **`viral-hooks`**. Writes the hook for the platform, using its platform table: three variants by default, each labeled with the levers it uses.
4. **`dumbify`**. About 6th-grade reading level for the hook and 8th-grade for the body, without flattening the rhythm.
5. **`revision-passes`**. For anything over about 150 words:
   - cut about 10% so nothing seems missing;
   - use Draft No. 4 boxes on the hook and the last line.
6. **`sentence-craft`**. Written long-form only (newsletters, long posts, threads): one sentence per line, the names of things, no forced logic words. Skip it for short captions.
7. **`worldbuilding`** / **`story-forge`**. Only when the post is itself fiction, or is set in an invented world.
8. **`style-audit`**, light mode. Usage errors only: it's/its, agreement, dangling modifiers, misused words. Never formalize fragments, contractions or casual style.
9. **`anti-ai-writing`**. Always last. The full pass on the caption and any written text, and the spoken subset on the script.

**Deliver in the platform's shape:**
- Reel: script, on-screen text and caption.
- Carousel: slide-by-slide text, with slide 1 at 9 words or fewer.
- Thread: the first tweet, then the rest.

## PROSE — essays, articles, emails, bios, speeches, cover letters

1. **`revision-passes`**. If a draft exists: structure, lead, ending, and a 10% cut. For a new piece, settle the lead and the ending first.
2. **`nonviolent-communication`**. When the message is hard (an apology, complaint, boundary or piece of feedback): observations, feelings, needs, a request.
3. **`storytelling`**. When the piece carries a story (newsletter essays, speeches, about pages): the but/therefore beats and the last line.
4. **`reader-brain`**. For narrative nonfiction: whose story, what's at stake, specifics.
5. **`sentence-craft`**.
6. **`dumbify`**. When the piece explains or teaches.
7. **`viral-hooks`**. The title, subject line or first line. Use its platform table for newsletters and the four killers everywhere else.
8. **`style-audit`**, full audit.
9. **`anti-ai-writing`**, full pass.

## PERSUADE — ads, sales pages, pitches, offers, launches, funnels, brand lines

1. **`storybrand`**. The one-liner: the customer is the hero, the brand is the guide, and there is one clear desire.
2. **`expert-secrets-playbook`**. The one belief the buyer must hold (its "Big Domino"), the story that installs it, and the two or three false beliefs to break.
3. **`100m-offers`**. When the piece presents an offer or price: the value equation, the stack, the guarantee, the name.
   - **`name-craft`** joins when a company, brand, product or program needs a name. The offer package's own name stays with `100m-offers`.
4. **`100m-money-models`**. When offers come in sequence: upsell, downsell, continuity.
5. **`sell-like-crazy`**. Funnel stage and traffic temperature: the lead magnet, the call to action, the follow-up.
6. **`storytelling`**. The story inside the pitch (reels, sales videos, founder stories).
7. **`viral-hooks`**. The headline or hook.
8. **`dumbify`**. Reading level.
9. **`style-audit`**. Light for social ads, full for sales pages and proposals.
10. **`anti-ai-writing`**. Last.

Every persuasion skill forbids fake scarcity, fake urgency, invented testimonials and guarantees nobody will honor. The room enforces that across the whole stack. Match the intensity to the setting: a formal proposal keeps the structure and drops the hype.

## NAME — names and titles

1. **Pick the skill.**
   - `name-craft` names things: businesses, brands, products, features, packages, events, imprints, teams, characters, places, ships, factions.
   - `title-craft` titles works: books and subtitles, chapters, series and their book-title patterns, articles, videos, newsletters, subject lines and listings.
2. **Brief or reader first.** `name-craft` writes the naming brief. `title-craft` finds the one reader and their problems, questions, roadblocks and results.
3. **Go long, then cut.** Both generate twenty to a hundred candidates and cut them with SMILE and SCRATCH. `title-craft` adds the promise and skim-path checks.
4. **`style-audit`**, light: spelling, capitals, italics for book titles, no stray punctuation.
5. **`anti-ai-writing`** on the rationale and any tagline. It never rewrites the names themselves.
6. **Deliver** the shortlist with one line of rationale each, then:
   - the acid-test sentence (names) or the skim path (titles);
   - the checks still to run: trademark, domain, tests.

## VIDEO — scripts and stories into shots

1. **`storytelling`**. The script: lens, beats, last line, length budget.
2. **`viral-hooks`**. The visual hook: the first 1–2 seconds, showing while telling.
3. **`dumbify`**.
4. **`anti-ai-writing`**. The spoken subset on the script and the full pass on the caption.
5. **`seedance-shotlist-director`**. Writes the HTML shotlist of 15-second prompts, each with the style prefix. If its default output folder doesn't exist in this environment, save the file in the working folder and say where.
