# Fiction Titles

## Name something solid

- An effective title points at something actually in the story: a person, a place, a theme or a genre. The strongest name several at once, as a one-word title can give the monster, the wild setting and the man-against-nature theme together. [McKee ch18]
- Don't use a literary non-title that hides what kind of story it is. A title that sounds like one genre when the story belongs to another sends the audience in the wrong direction. [McKee ch18]
- A title positions the audience. Shakespeare framed his plays as tragedies or comedies in their titles so playgoers knew what they'd come for, and a modern film whose title promised a murder mystery left viewers waiting for a death that never came. [McKee ch4]
- The title shines a light down into the story. It promises, and the story has to keep that promise. [McPhee, Structure]
- A title plants a preconception the reader carries to the end. A story titled for a delusion invites the reader to doubt the threat, and an ending that overturns the title unsettles them. [Uncanny, McKnight Hardy]

## Find it

- Start with a working title and expect a better one to arrive while you draft. If one never does, an editor will often supply it. [King, And Furthermore]
- Good lines announce themselves as you write, and they often become beginnings, endings or titles. Keep a list. [McPhee, Omission]
- Familiar phrases, songs and other books' titles are rich sources, and a title made from a phrase people already know arrives half-remembered. [Watkins ch5]
- A title that carries a picture is remembered. One that names an abstraction is forgotten. [Watkins ch1]
- Research what sells in your category. Look at the bestselling titles, their covers and their reviews, and notice the patterns they share. [Edwards S12]
- A hook (a quick story that sparks curiosity) usually lives in the tagline and the blurb; the title names, the tagline teases. [Edwards S29]

## Check it

- Say it aloud and imagine a friend recommending it. Readers who can't spell or pronounce a title can't search for it. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Run `name-craft`'s SCRATCH test. Spelling trouble, insider references and hard pronunciation sink titles as surely as brand names. [Watkins ch2]
- See it on the cover. The type and the length of the title must work at thumbnail size and in the genre's style. [Tomlinson ch6]
