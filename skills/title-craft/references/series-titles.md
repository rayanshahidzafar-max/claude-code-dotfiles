# Series Names and Book-Title Patterns

## Decide early

- Settle the series name and the book titles at the planning stage if you can. You can then weave nods to the titles through each book, and design the covers as a set. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Put working titles for every planned book in the series outline. [Tomlinson ch5]

## Name the series

- Series are commonly named after a character, the first book, a group in the story, or a place. Look at the series titles that sell in your genre and notice which pattern they use. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Choose a series name that's simple to say and to recall, so a reader who hears it can search for it. Avoid weird spellings, and remember it will be the smaller line on every cover. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Where you can, let the series title capture the premise or theme as a metaphor, the way a show about castaways named for being lost meant lost in every sense. [Tomlinson ch5]
- A series title can promise the confrontation to come, so readers feel suspense across every book. [Tomlinson ch16]
- Signal the genre. A punchy one-word series name was dropped because it could have belonged to anything; the longer name that said science fiction did the selling. [Tomlinson ch24]

## Pattern the book titles

- Book titles in a series can share a recurring word or theme, the same word count and shape, or a matching formula. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Titles can foreshadow, promising a return or a showdown before the book delivers it. [Scheuerer ch12]
- Once you choose a pattern, keep it for the whole series; consistency is the brand. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Test the pattern on the covers. One author had to retitle a book because it didn't fit the spacing of the earlier covers, and another found a two-word title broke his cover layout. [Tomlinson ch24]
- Don't pick a pattern so rigid it can't survive an extra book or a change of direction. A suffix family that fits two names may have nowhere to go at the third. [Watkins ch2]
- A theme with legs stretches across a whole line; choose one you won't exhaust. [Watkins ch1]

## Talk to both kinds of reader

- Readers who already know the series need its name and the book's number up front. New readers need the promise. Write the cover and the listing for both. [Edwards S7]
- Use the same words and look across every book, ad and page, so returning readers know at once that this is the same series. [Edwards, Q&A]
