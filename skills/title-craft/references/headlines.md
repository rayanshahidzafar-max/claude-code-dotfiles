# Headlines, Subject Lines, Posts, Videos and Listings

## The same job everywhere

- The title of a video, article or post is its headline. Give it the same care, because it decides whether anything else gets seen. [Edwards S6]
- Never publish without a headline or a strong first statement. If in doubt, lead with curiosity. [Edwards S6]
- Most people judge in seconds. Get to the point fast, with less warm-up than you think you need. [Edwards S1]
- In video, the first spoken words are the headline: grab attention, say who you are, say what's coming. [Edwards S17]
- Learn titles first and bullets second. Every piece of writing has a headline, so the skill pays everywhere. [Edwards S5]

## Match the reader's temperature

- Readers who don't know a solution exists respond to their problem. Readers looking for a solution respond to the solution. Readers who know you respond to your product by name. [Edwards S7]
- For cold readers a negative or fear-based headline usually works best, because it enters the worry already in their head. For warm readers lead with the solution, and for hot readers with the offer. [Edwards, Q&A]
- A generic "we help people do X" fits nobody. Name the specific sub-group and its specific want. [Edwards S7]

## Subject lines

- A subject line is the email's headline. If it fails, nothing else is read. [Edwards S18]
- The best are short, concise and often a question the right reader answers yes to. [Edwards S18]
- The email's only job is the click, so the subject line sells the opening, not the product. [Edwards S18]

## Ads and listings

- An ad's whole job is to pull clicks from the people it's for and let everyone else scroll past. [Edwards S28]
- Curiosity is the strongest click driver: make them ask what it is, or how it's done. [Edwards S28]
- When stuck, open with a question the right reader answers yes to: one about a pain they're sick of, a result they'd like, or something they've always meant to do. [Edwards S28]
- Online, three moves are enough: an emotional grab, a curiosity driver, and one clear call to action. [Edwards S28]
- Expect to write and test ten to fifty before one wins, and keep replacing them as they wear out. [Edwards S28]
- Test one variable at a time against the current winner, and measure actions (clicks, sales, sign-ups), not opinions. [Edwards S11]
- A title that works is the one to beat, not the end of the job. A single headline change once turned a product that sold nothing into a best week. [Edwards S6]
- Keep a swipe file by type (titles, chapter lists, subject lines) and read it to warm up before you write. The best finds often come from outside your field. [Edwards S30]

## Polish

- Skimmers read the headline, the subheads, the bold lines and the last line. Make sure that path alone tells the story. [Edwards S31]
- Proofread every title. A typo in a headline makes readers doubt everything under it. [Edwards S31]
- When a title underperforms, check in order: is it about the reader, does it carry a clear benefit or promise, and does it draw the right people rather than everyone? [Edwards S23]
- Spell out initials the reader may not know. [S&W V.19]
