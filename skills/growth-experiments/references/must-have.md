# Must-Have and the Aha Moment

## The must-have survey

- Ask active users how disappointed they'd be if the product went away. [Ellis ch2]
- If 40 percent or more say very disappointed, it's a must-have: go for growth. [Ellis ch2]
- Between about 25 and 40 percent, tweak the product or the language. [Ellis ch2]
- Below about 25 percent, you likely have the wrong audience or the wrong product. [Ellis ch2]
- Also ask what they'd use instead, the main benefit, who benefits most, and what to improve. [Ellis ch2]
- Survey active users, not everyone. [Ellis ch2]
- You need a few hundred responses; with fewer, interview instead. [Ellis ch2]
- Check retention against benchmarks too; a flattening retention curve signals fit. [Ellis ch2]
- A viral push before value can flame out, as one social app's did. [Ellis ch2]

## The aha moment

- The aha moment is when users first get the core value. [Ellis ch2]
- Examples: reading great reviews, following enough accounts, getting enough survey responses. [Ellis ch2]
- Compare avid users with everyone else to find the actions that predict staying. [Ellis ch2]
- Drive new users to aha fast, like connecting with several friends in the first days. [Ellis ch2]
- Treat the new-user experience as its own product. [Ellis ch2]
- Invalidate company lore: forced training once raised retention when everyone assumed it would hurt. [Ellis ch2]

## Get out of the building

- Talk to customers; don't sell during interviews. [Ellis ch2]
- Watch behaviour where it happens: craft fairs, campus groups. [Ellis ch2]
- Find existing communities where users already gather. [Ellis ch2]
- Ask returning users a few simple questions. [Ellis ch2]
- A/B test messaging cheaply before building. [Ellis ch2]
- Clicks are a poor proxy; track downstream behaviour. [Ellis ch2]
- Build hypotheses before costly product tests. [Ellis ch2]
- Unify data and track events. [Ellis ch2]
- Many famous products pivoted on what the data showed. [Ellis ch2]
- A marketer surveyed people who hadn't upgraded and found most didn't know a paid version existed. [Ellis ch1]
- Surfacing real positive reviews is a cheap "love hack". [Ellis ch1]
- Find the habitual users and the habit path they took, then steer new users down it. [Eyal ch8]
- Say "every time the user feels ___, they ___" to name the need. [Eyal ch2]
