# What the Rest of the Room Adds

## Habit as a growth engine (Eyal)

- Habit-forming products earn more per customer, hold price better and grow faster, because frequent users spread the word more often. [Eyal ch1]
- A newcomer must be many times better than the habit it replaces. [Eyal ch1]
- Frequency plus perceived usefulness puts a behaviour in the habit zone. [Eyal ch1]
- Owned triggers (an icon, a newsletter, notifications) bring users back without paying again. [Eyal ch2]
- Paid triggers acquire; they're too costly to re-engage. [Eyal ch2]
- Earned triggers like press fade fast. [Eyal ch2]
- Make the action easier rather than pushing motivation. [Eyal ch3]
- Rewards must fit why people use the product. [Eyal ch4]
- Stage investments from small to big, after the reward. [Eyal ch5]
- Load the next trigger in the investment. [Eyal ch5]
- Check the plan against the manipulation matrix: would you use it, and does it improve lives? [Eyal ch6]
- If fewer than about one in twenty users are habitual, fix the product before growth. [Eyal ch8]

## Testing creative and audiences (Kane)

- Write many variations per concept and test them cheaply. [Kane ch1]
- Separate each interest into its own test. [Kane ch1]
- Duplicate winning ad sets instead of raising budget. [Kane ch1]
- Every follower or user has a cost; know what they're worth. [Kane ch1]
- Retarget the engaged and build look-alikes from them. [Kane ch2]
- Use search trends to size and pick topics. [Kane ch4]
- Leading actions like shares mark the real audience. [Kane ch2]
- Growth compounds; it's a marathon. [Kane ch4]

## Making loops spread (Berger)

- A loop spreads faster when the shared thing gives the sharer social currency. [Berger ch1]
- Frequent everyday cues keep a product in conversation. [Berger ch2]
- High-arousal emotion drives sharing. [Berger ch3]
- Visible use spreads by imitation; build products that advertise themselves. [Berger ch4]
- Don't publicise how many people do the wrong thing. [Berger ch4]
- Deals are shared when they beat expectations; limits make them feel better. [Berger ch5]
- Paying people to share can crowd out their own reasons. [Berger ch1]

## Landing pages that convert (Sugarman)

- Every element exists to get the first sentence read. [Sugarman ch4]
- Get the reader nodding yes with true statements. [Sugarman ch7]
- Answer the next question before it's asked. [Sugarman ch16]
- Anticipate and resolve objections in the copy. [Sugarman ch18]
- Make ordering easy and ask for the order. [Sugarman ch18]
- A simple offer beats a complex one. [Sugarman ch19]
- Put buy buttons throughout a long page. [Sugarman ch36]
- Test small before big. [Sugarman ch10]

## Community as a channel (Schaefer)

- Most marketing now happens without the brand, in what people tell each other. [Schaefer, Intro]
- Community members bring new customers at a fraction of usual cost. [Schaefer ch6]
- People trust a community more than the company. [Schaefer ch3]
- Don't force direct attribution on community; it's brand marketing. [Schaefer ch10]

## Channels and native content (Vaynerchuk)

- Be first on new platforms and test. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Boost only what already performs organically. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Content must be native to each platform. [Vaynerchuk, Intro]
- Link straight to the exact page promised. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Give before you ask. [Vaynerchuk R2]
