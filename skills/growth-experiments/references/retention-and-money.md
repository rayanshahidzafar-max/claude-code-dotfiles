# Retention, Monetisation and Stalls

## Retention

- A small lift in retention brings a large lift in profit. [Ellis ch7]
- A home-cleaning startup's discounts attracted people who churned. [Ellis ch7]
- Retention compounds. [Ellis ch7]
- Treat retention in three stages: the first days, the middle months and the long haul. [Ellis ch7]
- Analyse cohorts by sign-up month, channel and behaviour. [Ellis ch7]
- A campaign that brings ill-fitting customers shows up as a steep cohort drop. [Ellis ch7]
- Build habits, as a shopping membership with free shipping does. [Ellis ch7]
- Rewards can be tangible or experiential: ambassador programmes, refer-ten perks. [Ellis ch7]
- Recognise achievements with milestone messages and tell people when others act on their things. [Ellis ch7]
- Personalise. [Ellis ch7]
- Tease what's coming soon. [Ellis ch7]
- Avoid feature bloat; beta new features with a small share of users. [Ellis ch7]
- Onboarding is ongoing: introduce features after the basics are mastered. [Ellis ch7]
- To resurrect lapsed users, learn why they left, separate what you can control, build flows by length of inactivity, and don't nag. [Ellis ch7]

## Monetisation

- Map the revenue funnel and find the pinch points, such as abandoned carts. [Ellis ch8]
- Look at revenue by cohort. [Ellis ch8]
- Ask segments what they'd pay for, and have them rank options. [Ellis ch8]
- Recommend what similar buyers bought, without being creepy. [Ellis ch8]
- Find the price range with four questions: too expensive, expensive but considered, a good deal, too cheap. [Ellis ch8]
- Fit pricing to each persona. [Ellis ch8]
- Use a value metric that scales with the value delivered. [Ellis ch8]
- Make comparison pages clear. [Ellis ch8]
- Test prices regularly, but keep each visitor's price consistent. [Ellis ch8]
- A decoy option makes the target option look better. [Ellis ch8]
- Raising a price can raise revenue. [Ellis ch8]
- The gap between free and a penny is the biggest friction; freemium upgrades work by showing locked features. [Ellis ch8]
- Persuasion: samples (reciprocity), wish lists (commitment), specific photo testimonials (social proof), authority, real faces (liking), genuine scarcity. [Ellis ch8]

## Avoiding stalls

- Growth stalls through complacency, dependence on one channel whose rules change, and distraction. [Ellis ch9]
- Keep swimming: set a minimum number of tests per week. [Ellis ch9]
- Double down on winners, like finding the rest of a ship in a game of battleships. [Ellis ch9]
- Deepen the data. [Ellis ch9]
- Add organic channels. [Ellis ch9]
- Open ideation to outsiders. [Ellis ch9]
- Test big moonshots like full redesigns now and then. [Ellis ch9]
