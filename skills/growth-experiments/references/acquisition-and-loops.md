# Acquisition and Loops

## Fit before scale

- Customer acquisition cost must stay below customer value. [Ellis ch5]
- First find language-market fit, then channel-product fit. [Ellis ch5]
- The message must land fast, like a music player sold as a thousand songs in your pocket. [Ellis ch5]
- A media site tested two headlines in similar cities to learn what worked. [Ellis ch5]
- Small word changes can have a huge effect. [Ellis ch5]
- Language fit can reshape the product and the brand. [Ellis ch5]

## Channels

- Don't spread across every channel; discover, then optimise a few. [Ellis ch5]
- Make the first cut by business model and user behaviour. [Ellis ch5]
- Score channels on cost, targeting, control, input time, output time and scale. [Ellis ch5]
- Keep trying new channels as old ones saturate. [Ellis ch5]

## Viral and referral loops

- Build loops only after must-have. [Ellis ch5]
- Word of mouth differs from a built-in loop; the loop is engineered into use. [Ellis ch5]
- A viral factor above one is rare. [Ellis ch5]
- Virality depends on payload (how many invites), conversion and frequency. [Ellis ch5]
- Invitations work best when delivered naturally through use, like a sign-up link added to every message a user sends. [Ellis ch5]
- Don't spam people's contacts. [Ellis ch5]
- Network effects make the product better as more people join. [Ellis ch5]
- The incentive should be the core value: extra storage, travel credit. A gift unrelated to the product is wrong. [Ellis ch5]
- A cloud storage referral that paid in storage raised sign-ups sharply. [Ellis, Intro]
- Integrate the invite into the experience. [Ellis ch5]
- Give invitees a good landing: the inviter's name and photo, and their reward. [Ellis ch5]
- Experiment with the invite flow, even asking for a few more invites. [Ellis ch5]
- Users can help build the product, as volunteers once translated a social network. [Ellis, Intro]

## Low-friction sharing and saveable assets

- Make sharing part of using the product, with as few steps as possible, and cut every step that isn't needed. [Ellis ch6]
- Make savings and achievements visible at the moment they happen. [Berger ch5]
- Package useful knowledge short and focused so it's easy to forward. [Berger ch5]
- Narrow, specific content is shared to the one friend it fits. [Berger ch5]
- Behavioural residue (a badge, a saved checklist, a template) keeps advertising after use. [Berger ch4]
- Make the sharer look good: insider knowledge, a clever tool, a result to show. [Berger ch1]
- Make the brand integral, so the asset can't be passed on without it. [Berger ch6]
- Utility is one of the three things people keep on their phones; useful assets earn a place. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Give fans something to give their friends. [Kane ch5]
- Share is the metric; a save is a quieter share. [Kane ch5]
- Help members share outside the community; it's the key metric after sales. [Schaefer ch10]
- Asset ideas that pass these checks: an infographic of one surprising number, a checklist, a calculator, a template, a before-and-after.
