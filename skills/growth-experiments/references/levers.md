# Levers, the Growth Equation and the Team

## The growth equation

- Pull the right lever at the right time; one photo-sharing startup died working on the wrong one. [Ellis ch3]
- Small teams should run high-impact tests first; be dramatic, since tiny tweaks need huge samples. [Ellis ch3]
- Write the growth equation, for example traffic times conversion times active rate, plus retained and resurrected users. [Ellis ch3]
- Key actions are the ones that correlate with core value. [Ellis ch3]
- Vanity metrics differ by business; daily active users mean little to a travel booking site. [Ellis ch3]
- The North Star metric best captures the core value delivered: messages sent, nights booked. [Ellis ch3]
- Expect to revise the North Star as the business matures. [Ellis ch3]
- A rental site found listings with professional photos booked better and built a photography programme. [Ellis ch3]
- Avoid analysis paralysis. [Ellis ch3]
- Instrument properly. [Ellis ch3]
- Numbers tell what, not why; interview people. [Ellis ch3]
- Keep dashboards accessible and avoid dumping data. [Ellis ch3]
- Interview users who came back after going dormant. [Ellis ch3]

## The team

- A growth lead sets the focus area and tempo and runs the weekly meeting. [Ellis ch1]
- The team includes product, engineering, marketing, a data analyst and a designer. [Ellis ch1]
- The cycle is analyse, ideate, prioritise, test. [Ellis ch1]
- It needs an executive sponsor. [Ellis ch1]
- Teams can sit inside product or stand alone. [Ellis ch1]
- Decide by data, not lore; success silences dissent. [Ellis ch1]
- Start small: one product, one channel, one metric. Get buy-in. Expect breakage. [Ellis ch1]
- Don't outsource growth. [Ellis ch1]
- Solo creator: run the same cycle alone, one hat at a time.
