# Growth Experiments

This skill grows a product or brand by running many small, scored experiments across the whole funnel. It produces the **growth board** (`references/growth-board.md`):
- the must-have check and the aha moment;
- the growth equation and the North Star metric;
- the focus lever for this cycle;
- the idea backlog, scored by ICE;
- this week's tests, with hypotheses and downstream metrics;
- the loop (referral, content or sharing) and its incentive;
- the lessons learned.

For audience and hook tests, use `audience-growth`. For the return habit, use `habit-hooks`. For what makes a shared asset spread, use `contagious-content`. For the offer itself, use `100m-offers`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- A great product doesn't spread itself; believing it will is a fallacy. [Ellis, Intro]
- Growth hacking means a cross-functional team running rapid experiments driven by data. [Ellis, Intro]
- It covers the whole funnel: acquisition, activation, retention and monetisation, not just acquisition. [Ellis, Intro]
- There's no silver bullet; growth comes from many small wins compounded. [Ellis, Intro]
- It isn't a lone genius, rule-breaking, or only marketing. [Ellis, Intro]
- Ads cost more and work less; marketing spend alone doesn't predict growth. [Ellis, Intro]
- Growth follows from people loving the product; it can't manufacture that love. Push a product people don't love and you spread the disappointment. [Ellis ch2]
- Don't scale before the product is a must-have. [Ellis ch1]
- The fastest learners grow fastest, and most experiments fail. [Ellis ch4]
- Asking people who left why can transform the message; many abandoned a product because they didn't believe it was really free. [Ellis, Intro]
- Habits make growth cheaper because heavy users return and spread the word more often. [Eyal ch1]

## Where the books disagree

- **Incentivise sharing?** Ellis pays referrals in core value; Berger warns that paying people to share can replace their own reasons. Resolution: reward in the product's own value, keep the reward secondary to the reason to share, and make sharing happen naturally through use.
- **Data or gut?** Ellis decides by data; Kane sometimes trusts instinct. Resolution: data for decisions, instinct for hypotheses.
- **Speed or quality?** High tempo can ship ugly tests; Vaynerchuk warns never to boost weak creative. Resolution: test fast, but only scale what passed.
- **Community metrics.** Ellis wants everything instrumented; Schaefer says community is brand marketing. Resolution: measure community with leading signals and don't force last-click attribution.
