# Platform Native

This skill turns one story into posts that belong on each platform, and spaces the asks so they land. It produces the **jab plan** (`references/jab-plan.md`):
- the brand story and the voice that stays constant;
- the jabs: what each gives, on which platform, in what native form;
- the right hook: one ask, built for mobile and the platform;
- the ratio and the calendar;
- the community-management plan for replies.

For the hook line, use `viral-hooks`. For the caption flow, use `slippery-copy`. For testing variations and paid boosts, use `audience-growth`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- Marketers chase the knockout punch, but right hooks miss unless jabs have set them up. [Vaynerchuk, Intro]
- A jab is one piece of engagement that gives the audience something and builds the relationship; a right hook is a call to action that benefits the business. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Content must be native to the platform it's on. [Vaynerchuk, Intro]
- The main task is to put your story in front of people where they already are, ideally at the moment they decide to buy. [Vaynerchuk, Intro]
- It takes hustle, heart, sincerity and constant engagement over the long term. [Vaynerchuk, Intro]
- Where and how a piece appears matters even more than the piece itself. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- People are on social platforms for value, not to buy. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Give, give, give, then ask. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Build so much goodwill that saying no to your ask feels rude. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- No sale happens without the story. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- The less you sell, the more you sell; keep about four parts giving to one part asking. [Kane ch5]

## How to use this in practice

1. **State the brand story** and the voice that stays the same everywhere: same person, different outfit for each room. → `references/rules-of-content.md` [Vaynerchuk R1]
2. **Break each platform into its building blocks:** text, image, tone, link capabilities. → `references/platforms.md` [Vaynerchuk R11]
3. **Plan the jabs:** each gives a laugh, a thought, a game, appreciation or an escape, one mental step from the product. → `references/jab-plan.md` [Vaynerchuk R2]
4. **Check each jab against the six rules.** → `references/rules-of-content.md` [Vaynerchuk R2]
5. **Write the right hook:** one simple call to action, built for mobile, true to the platform. → `references/right-hook.md` [Vaynerchuk R1]
6. **Space them:** jab, jab, jab, then the hook. Brands with strong equity jab less but never stop. [Vaynerchuk R2]
7. **Watch the conversation** and be ready to trendjack honestly. → `references/micro-and-trends.md` [Vaynerchuk R4]
8. **Answer people.** Micro-content plus community management is what works. → `references/micro-and-trends.md` [Vaynerchuk R2]
9. **Read the data** for timing and format, and boost only what already works. [Vaynerchuk R3]
10. **Keep going daily;** a campaign runs 365 days a year. [Vaynerchuk R1]

## Where the books disagree

- **How many jabs?** The curriculum says three jabs per right hook; Vaynerchuk says there's no fixed blueprint; Kane uses about four to one. Resolution: three to one as a starting default, then let engagement data set the ratio for each platform.
- **Repurpose or rebuild?** Kane cuts one piece into dozens of variations; Vaynerchuk mocks pasting the same ad everywhere. Resolution: cut and re-edit freely, but rebuild the format, length and opening for each platform so the piece looks born there.
- **Trendjacking.** Vaynerchuk rides trends in real time; Sugarman warns fads fade and Berger wants frequent triggers. Resolution: trendjack for reach, but tie the brand to a recurring cue for lasting recall.
- **Selling in captions.** Sugarman writes to sell; Vaynerchuk keeps the ask rare. Resolution: jabs don't sell; the right hook sells hard and simply.
