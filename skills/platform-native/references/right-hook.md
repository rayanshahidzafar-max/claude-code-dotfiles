# Building the Right Hook

## The three traits

- The call to action is simple and easy. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- It's crafted for mobile. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- It respects the platform's nuances. [Vaynerchuk R1]

## The checklist

- Is the text too long? [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Is the logo visible on the image itself? [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Is the ask where the eye lands? [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Does the link go straight to the exact page, not the homepage? A bait-and-switch link erodes trust. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- A picture post usually beats a link post. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Put the text in the centre so mobile cropping doesn't cut it. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- One call to action per post; two questions is too many. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Don't answer your own question. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Don't give the payoff in the post; leave it for the click. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Use a strong photo, never stock. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Ask "would I share this?" before posting. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Check the timing; an early-morning post can underperform. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Invest in professional photos when quality lifts engagement. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Social isn't lipstick for a bad product. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Target right hooks by segment and rewrite them for each. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Keep the voice and slang through to the ask. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- On a pin or product image, show the price and link to the exact product. [Vaynerchuk R5]

## The rest of the room

- Ask for the order clearly and make ordering easy. [Sugarman ch18]
- A simple offer beats a complex one. [Sugarman ch19]
- Give value, then pivot with "by the way". [Edwards S25]
- Make the ask to the people the jabs already warmed up, by retargeting them. [Kane ch5]
- Don't ask for an opt-in before explaining the value. [Ellis ch6]
