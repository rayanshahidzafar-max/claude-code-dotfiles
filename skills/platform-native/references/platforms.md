# Platform by Platform

## Facebook

- The algorithm rewards engagement (likes, comments, shares), not sales. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Jabs earn the engagement that makes the right hook reach more people. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Jab one step away from the product: a boot brand posts about weather, climbing or a show its fans love, found by asking them. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Polls invite easy engagement. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- Turn the "sawdust" of the business, the assets lying around, into content. [Vaynerchuk R3]

## X / Twitter

- Context beats content here. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- The mistake is treating it as a blog feed or for bragging. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Listening and giving get rewarded here. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Act like a news deejay: share the news with your opinion and spin. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Take a stand. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Search conversations near your topic and join them; office furniture can talk about deadlines, backache and happy hour. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Follow memes; don't try to create them. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- If you start a hashtag, make it distinct. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Don't pose cooler than you are; stay in your own voice. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- A small business can win with self-deprecation and humanity. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Surprise and delight a stranger and it earns you attention. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Use a brand voice, not a staffer's signature. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Cross-pollinate: use a big platform to grow a new one. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Three things matter: the platform's nuances, a distinct voice, and a business goal. [Vaynerchuk R4]

## Pinterest

- It's about aspiration and acquisition. [Vaynerchuk R5]
- Make every pin visually compelling, a collector's item. [Vaynerchuk R5]
- Treat boards as a storefront, and use them to show other sides of the brand. [Vaynerchuk R5]
- A price on the pin increases engagement; link to the exact product page. [Vaynerchuk R5]

## Instagram

- It's a consumption platform, like a magazine of images, and great for jabbing on tone. [Vaynerchuk R6]
- An occasional right hook in the caption is fine; too many calls to action reads as spam. [Vaynerchuk R6]
- Real, casual scenes beat glossy product shots and stock. [Vaynerchuk R6]
- Growth is slower: use shoutouts, get big accounts engaging, write captions that drive comments, show behind the scenes, start with smaller influencers. [Kane ch8]

## GIF and visual-culture platforms

- Moving images get more engagement through surprise and wonder. [Vaynerchuk R7]
- Platforms built on interests reward a custom look. [Vaynerchuk R7]

## Short video and new platforms

- Every platform will become social. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Restrictions breed creativity: in a few seconds of video, use cuts and edits, not one static shot. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Disappearing content gets focused attention. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Be first on new platforms and test. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Don't be a snob about what teenagers use. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Smart early movers ask how they can do better and can own a category for a year. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- On intimate short-video platforms, be real, allow mistakes, feature fans and post daily. [Kane ch10]
- Hook in three or four seconds and subtitle everything. [Kane ch5]

## LinkedIn

- Long copy is allowed; be serious, with some levity. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- It offers different value from the other platforms. [Vaynerchuk R8]
- Share simple weekly updates and questions that invite comments. [Kane ch11]
- Reach out with value first. [Kane ch11]

## YouTube

- Watch time, consistency and a clear point of view on one topic grow a channel. [Kane ch9]
- Measure follow-on views and give cues to keep watching. [Kane ch9]
