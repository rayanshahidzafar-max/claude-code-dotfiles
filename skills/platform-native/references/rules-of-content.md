# The Six Rules of Great Content

## 1. Native

- Format it wrong for the platform and it's doomed, however good it is. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Native means fluent, like dreaming in a language, not translating. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- TV ads only worked once they became native to TV: short scenes that told stories. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Native content mimics the platform's look, design and tone, and gives people what the surrounding posts give them. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Most brand content looks identical across platforms; you can't tell which one it was made for. That's the problem. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Turning a print ad into a TV ad by filming it would be absurd; pasting the same post everywhere is the same mistake. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Each platform has its own language; a brand posting the same thing everywhere is a tourist who won't learn the local words. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Done right, native content isn't a cheesy infomercial. The brand reads like a real person. [Vaynerchuk R2]

## 2. Doesn't interrupt

- Be the entertainment, not the break from it. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Hook in the first few seconds with no long intro. [Kane ch5]

## 3. Doesn't make demands often

- Make content simple, memorable, inviting to look at and fun to read. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Make it for the audience, not for yourself. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- People keep three kinds of things on their phone home screens: social (people), entertainment (escape) and utility (service). Content should be one of those. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Gentle jabs, one mental step from the product, work best. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- "Cool" means it hits the emotional centre so hard you must share it, and sharing says something about you. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- People on social want entertainment, information, connection and a chance to brag. [Vaynerchuk R2]

## 4. Leverages pop culture

- Each generation is shaped by its pop culture; integrate the brand into that stream. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Tie the message to what's already popular: news, entertainment, comedy, pets. [Kane ch3]

## 5. Micro

- Make tiny pieces, reimagined daily, responding to culture in real time. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- A cookie brand's quick post during a stadium blackout worked because the team was ready, it was on-brand, and it had no call to action. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Jab every day. [Vaynerchuk R2]

## 6. Consistent and self-aware

- Every post should answer "who are we?" [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Own your content and relationships rather than renting them. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Stay true to the brand: same person, different outfit for each room. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- The mistake is insisting the tone be identical everywhere. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- Small firms can move fast and keep their humour. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- A consistent persona that takes a stand sells; the middle doesn't. [Edwards S21]
