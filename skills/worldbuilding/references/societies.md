# Societies: The Ladder, the Bargain, the Merger

## The ladder

| Type | Size | Settlement | Kinship | Leadership | Economy | Conflict resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Band | a few to ~80 | none fixed | one extended family | informal, egalitarian | reciprocal sharing | kin mediate |
| Tribe | hundreds | fixed villages | several clans | a "big-man" who persuades but holds no office | reciprocal exchange | everyone is related; communal talk |
| Chiefdom | thousands to tens of thousands | one or more villages | ranked lineages | hereditary chief with a monopoly on force and information | redistribution: tribute in, feasts and stores out | the chief decides |
| State | tens of thousands and up | cities | territory, not kin | king or officials; professional bureaucrats in layers | taxes, specialists, often slaves | laws, judges, police |

[Diamond ch14]

Notes for building:

- **Everyone knowing everyone** stops working at a few hundred people. Above that, strangers must deal with strangers, and something has to keep them from killing each other. [Diamond ch14]
- **Chiefdoms** are where people first had to meet strangers regularly without violence. The answer was a chief with the sole right to use force, visible marks of rank, and ritual deference from commoners. [Diamond ch14]
- **States** are organized by territory, not kinship. They're often multiethnic and multilingual, and they can ask citizens to die for them, which was unthinkable in a band or tribe. [Diamond ch14]
- **Pairs of strangers** multiply much faster than people do. Twenty people form under two hundred pairs; two thousand form about two million. Each pair is a possible feud. That's why big societies centralize. [Diamond ch14]
- **Centralization** also follows from the need for decisions (everyone can't meet), redistribution (goods must move between regions), and density. [Diamond ch14]

## The kleptocrat's bargain

Every ranked society both serves its people and skims from them. The difference between a statesman and a thief is one of degree: how much of the tribute the elite keeps, and how much the commoners like what's done with the rest. [Diamond ch14]

Elites keep their position with some mix of four tools. For each society in your world, decide which it leans on. [Diamond ch14]

1. **Disarm the people and arm the elite.**
2. **Redistribute tribute in popular ways:** feasts, works, protection.
3. **Keep order.** The monopoly on force cuts the violence that plagues stateless life, which is a real service.
4. **An ideology or religion that justifies it all:** the chief as descendant of the gods, the king as the only one who can bring the rain.

Institutional religion also does two jobs for big societies: it binds unrelated strangers into one "us," and it gives people a reason to die for others. [Diamond ch14]

Revolts often come from inside, led by a younger brother or a rival who promises less oppression. [Diamond ch14]

For story, chart the society's own central value as it declines. Justice falls to unfairness, then to injustice, then to tyranny dressed as law, where might makes right and still calls itself legal. The bottom rung is where a kleptocracy's ideology has fully taken over. [McKee ch14]

## How societies merge

- Societies never merge because free small groups calmly vote to. Mergers happen only under the threat of an outside force, or by conquest. [Diamond ch14]
- Competition pushes each level toward the next: tribes join or conquer tribes to become chiefdoms, chiefdoms become states, states become empires. The larger unit wins only if it can handle the problems that come with size: pretenders, resentment, integration. [Diamond ch14]

## What happens to the defeated

What happens to the losers depends on population density. [Diamond ch14]

| Density | The defeated… |
|---|---|
| Sparse (bands) | move away; there is room to flee |
| Moderate (tribes) | men are killed, women taken, land occupied; there's no surplus to make captives worth keeping |
| Dense (chiefdoms, states) | are enslaved, or left in place to pay tribute and absorbed into the victor |

## The Polynesian gradient

Sibling societies from one ancestor can land at every rung in a few centuries. The deciding factors are island size, how broken up the land is, isolation, and how much food can be grown. [Diamond ch2]

- Where the land is small and cut off, the result is egalitarian villages with chiefs who live like everyone else.
- On large fertile islands, the result is ranked lineages, chiefs who marry only chiefs, forced labor on monuments, and fleets of conquest.

Use this when one culture has scattered across a world.
