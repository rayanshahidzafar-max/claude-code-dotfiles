# The Causal Chain

Work through the links in order. For each one, answer the questions and write one sentence of cause: "X is so because Y." A link with no cause is where clichés breed.

## 1. Geography

- **Axis and spread.** Which way does the land run?
  - Along the same latitude (or whatever sets the seasons and day length in your world), crops, animals, techniques and ideas spread fast, because conditions stay the same.
  - Across latitudes, they crawl or stop.
  - Map where things can travel easily and where they can't. [Diamond ch10]
- **Barriers.** Name them: deserts, mountain spines, narrow isthmuses, shifts in rainfall pattern, open ocean. Each one slows or filters what crosses. [Diamond ch10]
- **The island variables.** For each region, note six things. [Diamond ch2]
  1. Climate.
  2. The kind of ground: poor or rich, stone for tools or none.
  3. Coastal and sea resources.
  4. Area.
  5. How broken up the terrain is.
  6. Isolation.
- **Fragmentation.** A coast full of peninsulas and big islands breeds many rival states. A smooth, connected land breeds one. [Diamond, Epilogue]
  - Many rivals means an idea refused in one place can find a sponsor in another.
  - Unity means one decision can shut a door for everyone.
- **Connectedness.** Technology may move fastest at moderate connectedness: enough contact to share ideas, enough division to keep competing. [Diamond, Epilogue]

## 2. Starting resources

- **What could be harnessed?** Only a few of the wild species (or resources) in any region can be turned to use. The limit lies in the local stock, not in the people. [Diamond ch8]
- **The Anna Karenina filter.** To be harnessed, a candidate must pass *every* test; failing any single one disqualifies it. For animals, Diamond's tests are:
  - an efficient diet;
  - fast growth;
  - willingness to breed in captivity;
  - a manageable temper;
  - no tendency to panic;
  - herd social structure with a hierarchy humans can take over.

  Invent your world's tests. [Diamond ch9]
- **Head start.** Regions that started with more candidates, lost fewer to extinction, and had more that passed the filter get a head start that compounds. [Diamond ch9]
- **Domestication is usually unconscious.** People favor the bigger, sweeter or calmer individuals without meaning to, and over generations the harnessed thing changes. Nobody planned it. [Diamond ch7]

## 3. Surplus, storage and density

- **The chain.** Producing food, or whatever sustains bodies, yields more per unit of land, so the population grows. Settling down shortens the gap between births. [Diamond ch4]
- **Storage creates classes.** Stored surplus feeds people who don't produce food: rulers, bureaucrats, soldiers, priests, craftspeople, scribes. [Diamond ch4]
- **Beasts of labor** multiply the effect. They give food, fertilizer and traction, move goods overland, and in war are worth armies. [Diamond ch4]
- **Adoption is gradual.** The shift to farming wasn't a single decision. It came from countless small choices about how to spend a day. Prestige mattered as much as calories: the hunter who wanted the glory of the big kill. [Diamond ch6]
- **Autocatalysis.** Once it starts, it speeds up: density demands more food, and more food allows more density. Neighbors who don't adopt it get displaced, or adopt it in self-defense. [Diamond ch6]
- **The divergence test.** A people cut off from their old way of feeding themselves may go backward, becoming smaller, simpler and more peaceable. That can be a choice made to survive in a closed place. [Diamond ch2]

## 4. Society type and power

- Place each society on the ladder: band, tribe, chiefdom, state. Size and density decide which rungs are possible. See `societies.md`. [Diamond ch14]
- Write down how its elite keeps power: disarming the populace, redistributing tribute, keeping order, or an ideology that justifies it. [Diamond ch14]

## 5. Writing and information

- **Who invented writing, and who borrowed it?** Inventing writing from nothing is very rare. Most societies copied a working system (the blueprint), or heard only that writing existed and built their own (the idea). [Diamond ch12]
- **What is writing for?** Early writing serves the palace and the temple: accounts, rations, taxes, propaganda. Its difficulty keeps it with a small class of scribes, and that restriction suits the rulers. [Diamond ch12]
- **Can it be lost?** Yes. A script can die with the state that kept it. [Diamond ch12]
- **Who has the long memory?** A literate society inherits the recorded experience of distant places and past centuries. A society without writing knows only what its living members saw. That gap decides who walks into traps. [Diamond ch3]

## 6. Technology

- The device often comes before the need: tinkerers build things out of curiosity, and uses turn up later. The lone-genius story hides long chains of predecessors. See `contact.md` for adoption, spread and loss. [Diamond ch13]
- Technology builds on itself, speeding up because each advance enables the next and new pieces recombine into new tools. [Diamond ch13]

## 7. Contagion

- Crowd diseases need big, dense, connected populations to survive. They spread fast, run their course quickly, and leave survivors immune. They usually jump from the animals people live closest to. [Diamond ch11]
- A population that has lived with its own plagues for generations carries resistance, and carries the plagues. Isolated peoples have neither. [Diamond ch11]

## 8. Deep time

- **Fragile ground.** An early leader on fragile ground may destroy its own base through cleared forests and eroded soil, and power drifts toward sturdier lands. [Diamond, Epilogue]
- **Primacy fades.** Past primacy is no guarantee of future primacy. Decide who used to lead, and what they tell themselves about it now. [Diamond, Epilogue]
