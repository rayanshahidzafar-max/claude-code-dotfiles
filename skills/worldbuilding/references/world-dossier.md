# World Dossier Template

Fill only the sections the story needs. The last section, pressure points, is mandatory: it's what turns a setting into a story.

```
WORLD DOSSIER — <world / region>, for <story title>

SCOPE: what this story needs (one city / one border / one era / whole world)
SETTING: period · duration · location · level of conflict

1. GEOGRAPHY
   Axis of easy spread:
   Barriers (and what each one filters):
   Fragmentation (many rivals or one power?):
   Isolation:

2. STARTING RESOURCES
   What could be harnessed here, and what failed the filter (and on which test):
   Head start or handicap versus neighbours:

3. SURPLUS & DENSITY
   What sustains bodies; how it is stored:
   Who does not produce it (specialists):
   Population and density:

4. SOCIETIES (one row each)
   name | ladder rung | size | power held by | how they keep it (4 tools) | ideology | writing? | signature technology

5. INFORMATION
   Who writes, what for, who reads:
   What each society believes about the others (and where that belief came from):

6. CONTAGION
   Crowd plagues / native fevers / immunity map:

7. DEEP TIME (timeline, one line per era)
   era | who led | why | how the lead was lost | what the losers tell themselves now

8. COLLISIONS
   pair | proximate advantages | ultimate causes | information gap | fate of the defeated

9. WILD CARDS
   The unexplained cultural quirk:
   The individual whose choice bent a local outcome:

10. RULES THAT BIND THE WRITER
   The one great leap from reality (if any), then the rules that follow from it:

11. PRESSURE POINTS (mandatory)
   For each: the world's cause → the place it lands on one person's life → the choice it forces
```

Notes:

- **Pressure points.** Each pressure point should tie a far-off cause to one person's specific trouble. A world force the protagonist never feels is wasted on the page. [Cron ch6]
- **Rules that bind.** Section 10 is a contract with the reader. Once written, it can't be broken without cheating the reader. [McKee ch2]
- **The telling detail.** For each society, note one specific, surprising detail that makes it feel real, the kind of detail that makes a world convincing. [McKee ch8]
- **Contested chronicles.** Put every society's own chronicle beside what actually happened. Where they differ is where the story can live. [Diamond ch3]
- **The iceberg.** Most of the dossier never reaches the page. Knowing it, and leaving it out, is what gives the visible part its weight. [McPhee, Omission]
- **The world's own touchstones.** In-world legends, places and famous events work like allusions. Set each one up before you lean on it, and never lean on a real-world reference to do the work. [McPhee, Frame of Reference]
- **Continuity.** Check every draft against sections 1–10: every name, date, distance and rule. Anything that contradicts the dossier gets fixed in one or the other. [McPhee, Checkpoints]
- **Need to know.** If a section doesn't lead to a pressure point, it stays in the notes. [Cron ch2]

## Applying the engine to non-human or mechanical worlds

Diamond's logic is about causes, so it carries over to any world where bodies need sustaining, crowds gather, and knowledge travels. This table is a working translation for this skill, not a claim from the book.

| Diamond's variable | Ask instead, for a non-human world |
|---|---|
| Food / calories | What sustains a body here (energy, fuel, current), and can it be stored? |
| Domesticable species | What resources, processes or lesser machines can be harnessed? What filter do the candidates fail? |
| Latitude / day length | What sets the conditions a thing is tuned to (orbit, radiation band, grid frequency), and along which lines does it stay constant? |
| Crowd diseases | What contagion needs dense, connected populations (viral code, corrosion, a plague of minds)? Who carries resistance? |
| Writing | How are records kept, who can read them, and who controls the archive? |
| Band → state | Does the same ladder hold for minds that can copy or merge? What replaces kinship as the first bond? |
