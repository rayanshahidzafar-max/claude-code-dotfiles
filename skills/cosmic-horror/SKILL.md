# Cosmic Horror

This skill writes horror that comes from the nature of things: the universe, the mind, the self. It isn't about a monster in the cellar. It produces the **dread sheet** (`references/dread-sheet.md`), which goes into the story folder's design file:
- the violation at the story's centre;
- the defenses the story will strip;
- the atmosphere and the place;
- what will be glimpsed and what stays unnamed;
- what dies;
- an ending with no back door.

`story-design` turns the sheet into structure, and `story-forge` drafts it.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's story.

**About the source:** Ligotti's book is a pessimist's essay as well as a horror writer's. Use its philosophy as story material, an engine for dread, never as a message to the reader. Ligotti himself says a bleak philosophical system works better as a symbol inside fiction than as an explanation of the world. [Ligotti, Solutions]

## The core ideas

- All supernatural horror rests on one confusion: the proper order of things and the impossible bleed into each other. Something that should not be has come to be, and it can't be undone. [Ligotti, Preface]
- The deepest horror doesn't come from wicked people. It comes from existence itself, or from what consciousness does to the people trapped in it. [Ligotti, Supernaturalism]
- In cosmic horror the universe is inscrutable and run by forces that neither know nor care about us. Human beings are insignificant in it, and no hidden order is offered as consolation. [Ligotti, Supernaturalism]
- Atmosphere comes first and events second. A weird story crystallizes a mood; its events are impossible anyway, and its truth lies in the mood. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- Like the value of a life, atmosphere in horror flows backward from death. A threat the story never follows through on shrinks everything set up before it. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- Meaning lives on the brink of knowing. Name the mystery, make it a fact or an object, and it dies. [Ligotti, Thinking Horror notes]
- Readers of supernatural horror expect a death and feel cheated without one. It can be the death of a body, a mind, an identity, an ideal, or a whole idea of the universe. [Ligotti, Plot]
- The darkest vision needs the most dazzling language. [Ligotti, Sickness]
- The horror story's value depends on its people. Cosmic horror reduces human beings, and the reduction only hurts if the reader believes in them. [King, On Writing]
- A story is only as compelling as the forces against it. In cosmic horror those forces are the nature of things, pushed to the last step of the value's decline, where consciousness becomes something worse than death. [McKee ch14]

## How to use this in practice

1. **Name the violation.** In one line, write what should not be and yet is. *The town's dead keep their appointments.* Everything else grows from it. [Ligotti, Plot]
2. **Choose the source of the dread** with "What should not be" below: the puppet, the self, knowledge, the indifferent cosmos, the body in pain, the familiar turned wrong.
3. **Map the lead's defenses** with the table below, and decide the order in which the story strips them. [Ligotti, Philosophy]
4. **Choose the place and let atmosphere lead.** Prefer one unchanging locale where every character is caught in the same snare. → `references/atmosphere.md` [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
5. **Design the revelation as imminent.** Decide what the reader glimpses, what the characters piece together, and what is never named. → `references/revelation.md` [Ligotti, Thinking Horror notes]
6. **Decide what dies:** a body, a mind, an identity, a belief, or the lead's idea of the universe. Let the atmosphere be set by it from page one. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
7. **Design the ending before drafting.** It must keep the opening's promise, with no back door. → `references/endings.md` [Ligotti, Bleakness]
8. **Draft in febrile, concrete prose,** then audit every figure for concreteness, not plainness. → `references/febrile-prose.md`
9. **Fill in the dread sheet** and paste it into `02-design.md`.

## What should not be

- **The puppet.** A puppet is an effigy of us that knows nothing. When one seems to come alive, perception splits, and the split is supernatural horror. The anxiety of lifeless things taking on human qualities runs deep. [Ligotti, Preface]
- **The thing moved from elsewhere.** A puppet moves by a force outside it, speaks in a voice from somewhere else, and takes orders from behind and beyond. A character who discovers that this is true of himself has found the horror. [Ligotti, Preface]
- **The self-conscious nothing.** To be aware and to be nothing at once is a monstrosity, a chimera like the undead. [Ligotti, Preface]
- **Knowledge that can't be unlearned.** A new awareness arrives (fear of things not present to the senses, visions from within), the world stops knowing the knower, and there is no way back. [Ligotti, Beginnings]
- **The self that isn't there.** Up close, a self is fragments that only look whole from a distance. Bring the camera in on a character until the whole cracks. [Ligotti, Hypocrisy]
- **The latticework dissolving.** When emotion drains away, the sense that anything is organized, or makes sense at all, goes with it. The self turns out never to have been solid. [Ligotti, Depression]
- **Pain without a frame.** The word "evil" keeps a distance between being and suffering; there is something to fight. Pain closes the distance. Horror with no evil to defeat, only pain, is bleaker. [Ligotti, Philosophy]
- **The cosmic joke.** Turn the creation story upside down: humanity as the accident or the jest of older, indifferent beings. [Ligotti, Thinking Horror notes]
- **The experiment gone wrong** is a mainstay of horror, from Frankenstein on. [Ligotti, Bleakness]
- **Merriment beside the abyss.** The most horrible laughter belongs to people who stay cheerful while fully aware of the horrible: a crowd of puppets filing onto a pleasure cruise. [Ligotti, Facing Horror notes]
- **Fear that never runs out.** Nobody can be sure of their own reality, let alone of gods or invaders. Horror never runs short of material, because the real question is whether we are real. [Ligotti, Thinking Horror notes]
- **Explained, unexplained, or undecided.** McKee splits horror three ways: a source that is astounding but rational in the end (a maniac, a made monster), a source from beyond the natural world, and the super-uncanny, which never lets the audience settle on either. Cosmic horror usually lives in the third. [McKee ch4]
- **The threat moves closer.** The genre's history moves the menace inward: from the stranger, to the spouse and the neighbour, to the self, and to whatever may climb out of one's own unconscious. [McKee ch4]
- **The familiar turned wrong.** The uncanny is what was hidden in the familiar coming into the open: the home, the face, the reflection. [Uncanny, Intro]
- **The marionette's grace.** Puppets have a grace we lost by becoming conscious. A perfect, soulless, clockwork figure repels everyone except the one who has fallen for it. [Uncanny, Jarvis]

## The defenses the story strips

Ligotti borrows four defenses from Zapffe, the ways people keep the full knowledge of their condition out of mind. Give the lead all four, then take them away. [Ligotti, Philosophy]

| Defense | In the lead's life | How the story breaks it |
|---|---|---|
| Isolation | refusing to think about it; a household's agreed silence | the fact won't stay out of the room |
| Anchoring | faith, nation, family, law, profession: the official papers of a life | the anchor proves forged, or answers to the thing |
| Distraction | work, screens, hobbies, noise | the distraction fails, or starts to show the thing |
| Sublimation | turning the dread into art, theory or a career | the art becomes the door: the researcher, the painter, the writer |

- A character whose four defenses fail one after another has an arc of their own. A great writer's late crisis ran this way. [Ligotti, Consuming Horror notes]
- At the end of a cosmic story the lead can use none of the four. Everyone else goes about their business, and a downcast atmosphere hangs over everything for the one who knows. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- Walk the lead's main value down the ladder, act by act: consciousness, then unconsciousness, then death, then something worse than death. Or communication, then alienation, then isolation, then madness. [McKee ch14]

## Where the books disagree

- **Dark ending or up ending?** Ligotti says never promise darkness and then slip out the back with affirmation; atmosphere flows backward from death. McKee wants the counter-idea argued at full strength and an ending charge that is earned. Highsmith notes that the public likes wrongdoers caught. Series writers want readers to come back. Resolution used here: the opening's promise governs the ending. A cosmic story pays in dread. Any light at the end must be dramatized as a cost, never as a back door.
- **Awe or despair?** Dyson's Aickman shows that facing the unknowable honestly can readmit awe, even hope. Ligotti finds existence malignantly useless. Resolution: both are honest if they are earned. Cosmic horror ends in dread. A strange tale may end in awe, and that is a choice to make at design time, not a mood to drift into.
- **Febrile or plain?** Ligotti defends feverish, metaphor-rich prose for nightmare. S&W want plain, definite, concrete language. Klinkenborg wants plain sentences, and King cuts adverbs. Resolution: intensity must stay concrete. Ban vague horror adjectives ("indescribable," "eldritch," "unspeakable") as substitutes for images. "Unnamable" is allowed only where the prose dramatizes language failing. `style-audit` applies the same ruling.
- **How much explanation?** Ligotti says name the mystery and it dies, and offering an underlying order is the mistake. McKee says close holes and never cheat with a deus ex machina. Cron says the reader needs cause and effect. Resolution: explain the mechanics of events, how the characters got from here to there, and never explain the cosmos, what the thing is. Leave the abyss at the centre.
- **Plot or discover?** King starts from a what-if and lets the people try to work free, without plotting. Ligotti wants the ending to keep the opening's promise, which means knowing it. Resolution: discover the events, but fix the ending's terms (what dies, and no back door) before drafting.
- **Villain or universe?** McKee builds antagonism toward a dramatized "negation of the negation." Ligotti puts the horror in existence, not in bad men. Resolution: cosmic horror replaces the villain with pressure from the nature of things, but McKee's value ladder still structures the descent.
