# Atmosphere, Place and Mood

## Where atmosphere comes from

- Atmosphere began when our ancestors read portents into moonlit shadows and rustling leaves. The bare kit (the moon, shadows, the lonely place) still prepares the entrance of the abomination. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- On film, atmosphere is a costume from the wardrobe department. On the page it comes from inside the writer: their sensations, memories, emotions and body. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- The great horror writers don't pick an atmosphere off the shelf. It picks them, and it is as personal as a fingerprint. Find yours in your own bad dreams and moods, not in a predecessor's furniture. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- A weird story should crystallize one definite human mood, the mood of a person reaching against laws that won't bend. Events serve the mood, never the other way round. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]

## Integrate atmosphere with action

- In weak horror, atmosphere vanishes when the monster arrives so the action can start. Keep them fused: no cutting from the dark landscape to a jokey police station. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- The strongest settings are single and unchanging (one room, one house, one building, one asylum, one forest), where atmosphere is always present and every character is caught in the same snare. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- Choose a place that can make the atmosphere real: a house whose decay mirrors its owners, a locale whose fate is bound to the people in it. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]

## Three lessons from the tradition

- **The explained supernatural shrinks a story.** An early Gothic novelist explained every ghost away, so her heroines never faced metaphysical madness, only the smaller horror of marrying a bad man. She also never followed her threats through with a death, which retroactively drained her settings of their dread. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- **Anchor the atmosphere in death from the first paragraph.** Poe's ruined house is not picturesque but dispiriting. From the opening the reader expects no rescue, and the house and its last occupants go down together. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- **An abstract opening can be atmospheric** when it promises something worse than physical death. Lovecraft's most famous story opens with a reflection on the mercy of a mind that can't connect what it knows. Its narrator then pieces the knowledge together and finds even spring flowers poisoned for him. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]

## The tragic ground note

- Treat the horror as the pedal tone under everything. Beauty and love are passing grace notes over it, and a young couple's happiness only readies them for the grinder. [Ligotti, Tragedy]
- Horror begins at home: in the family, the marriage, the first house. [Ligotti, Tragedy]

## Sensory method from the other books

- Build atmosphere through every sense, and a sixth: the smell of a room, a cough from upstairs, the colour of the wallpaper, the sense, on a still estate, that the trees might topple in on the house. [Highsmith ch7]
- A brand-new house can feel haunted, not by a presence on the stairs but by gloom and tragedy still to come. Dread can point forward. [Highsmith ch7]
- The eerie belongs to places: empty landscapes, abandoned structures, deserted streets that still show signs of life (parked cars, the glow of television, a security light tripped by a fox). Evidence of people, without people, makes the reader the unwanted outsider. [Uncanny, Budden]
- A loop can be the horror. A scene that repeats exactly (the same wave, the same bell, footprints that won't wash away) feels sealed off from the rest of the world. [Uncanny, Royle]
- Every sensory detail must earn its place by telling the reader something the story needs. Description that carries no meaning is sightseeing. [Cron ch6]
