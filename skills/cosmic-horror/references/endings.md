# Endings Without a Back Door

## Keep the opening's promise

- Beware the bleak book that opens on a vision of darkness and closes by slipping out the back door onto a sunlit path. Readers of dark fiction learn to check the last page first. [Ligotti, Bleakness]
- Dark stories often betray themselves in the final paragraphs, with a closing voice that tells the audience the world is worth fighting for after all. Cut that voice. [Ligotti, Consuming Horror notes]
- Mass-market horror is pushed toward reassurance because publishers fear depressing the customer. The writers who are the bedrock of the genre resolved their stories darkly. [Ligotti, Theme]

## The shape of a cosmic ending

- **Don't destroy the world.** That is too simple and too merciful. Leave the world running, its people going about their business, with a pall over everything for the one who knows. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- **The lead can't unknow it.** The ending finds the lead unable to isolate, anchor, distract or sublimate any longer. [Ligotti, Atmosphere]
- **Theme shows in the resolution.** Two novels on the same theme (a self dissolving) part ways at the end.
  - One ends in serene transcendence, which is a modern deus ex machina.
  - The other dramatizes the dissolution affectingly: a persecuted man cooperates with the conspiracy, wakes as the person he replaced, sees himself at his own bedside, and screams. [Ligotti, Theme]
- **Dramatize it; don't label it.** A character calling his plight a "horrible drama" in passing conveys nothing. Put the nightmare on the page. [Ligotti, Theme]
- **The outsider's ending.** The characters get nothing for their suffering except its mark. Going on, if they go on, is gore without glory and madness without deliverance. [Ligotti, Theme]
- **Loops and returns.** A supernatural story can end where it began, with the victim becoming the next occupant of the same room and the cycle restarting. [Ligotti, Theme]

## Support from the other books

- The climax must change things absolutely and irreversibly. In cosmic horror, that change is usually inside the lead: what they now know. [McKee ch13]
- Without the last step down (past death, past madness, the stage McKee calls the negation of the negation), a story can satisfy but never astonish. [McKee ch14]
- **No reset button.** A story that undoes its own horror at the end has no consequence. Let the change stand. [Uncanny, Shearman]
- **The false reset.** Offer an escape route (it was the lenses pressing a nerve), then let the last image show what the escape cost. The character who chose ignorance has become the prey. [Uncanny, Shearman]
- **The circle.** A ghost story that ends with its first paragraph gives entropy instead of catharsis. The loop is the horror. [Uncanny, Ward]
- **The human chain stays clear.** Each event in the ending must follow from the one before, and the reader must be able to trace what the people decided and why, even while what the thing is stays dark. [Cron ch8]
