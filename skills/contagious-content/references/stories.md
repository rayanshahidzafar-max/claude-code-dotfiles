# Stories and Valuable Virality

## Stories carry the message

- Like the wooden horse at Troy, a story carries something inside it. The listener attends to the tale and the lesson comes along. [Berger ch6]
- People think in narratives, not in information. Even product reviews turn into little stories about a trip or a family. [Berger ch6]
- Because a narrative runs from opening to close, a listener who gets drawn in stays until it resolves. [Berger ch6]
- A tale of a man who lost a great deal of weight eating sandwiches taught people several things about the sandwich shop without anyone setting out to promote it. [Berger ch6]
- A short film that exposed how a model's photo is altered gave people a reason to talk about a sensitive subject, and the brand came along with it. [Berger ch6]
- Open with a short, offbeat story or a strange news item tied to the product; it pulls readers into the copy. [Sugarman ch8]
- A story should catch attention, connect to what is being sold and bond the teller to the listener. [Sugarman ch19]
- Unexpected outcomes at unexpected moments keep a story fresh, and a twist at the end makes people want to send it on. [Kane ch5]

## Make virality valuable

- A stunt nobody can link to the brand is a wasted horse. A diver in costume at the Olympics got the world talking and the sponsor nothing. [Berger ch6]
- Cute babies on roller skates went viral, and the water brand behind them lost share, because babies had nothing to do with water. [Berger ch6]
- The goal is valuable virality: the brand's benefit so woven into the story that people can't tell it without naming it. [Berger ch6]
- A blender destroying everything put in it can't be described without the blender, and its toughness is exactly the message. [Berger ch6]
- A cheese commercial whose punchline is the mascot works because the mascot is the joke. [Berger ch6]
- As a story is retold it gets shorter and sharper around its key details. Make the brand one of those key details, not scenery. [Berger ch6]
- Check: if someone retells the story in one breath, is the product still in it? If not, rebuild the story. [Berger ch6]
- Adjacent moments around the product (the half-time show, the dolphin riding the wave) can outperform the product itself, as long as they still belong to its world. [Kane ch5]
- Tell the story from the customer's side. They want to see themselves in it, not hear about you. [Edwards S4]
- A good story makes the reader feel it's about them. [Edwards S29]

## Putting it together

- Any product, idea or behaviour can catch on, and the principles can be built into the product or into the message around it. [Berger, Epilogue]
- No huge budget or creative genius is needed; ordinary people built several of the most shared ideas by leaning on one or two principles. [Berger, Epilogue]
- Use the six principles as a checklist before launch, not after. [Berger, Epilogue]
- Content that people share has to be worth their friends' time. Bait that disappoints damages the brand's next post. [Ellis ch5]
- Viral pushes only work on things people already love. Spreading a product before it's must-have spreads the disappointment too. [Ellis ch2]
- Spreading by word of mouth still needs the product to deliver. Habit and repeat use keep people talking long after the launch. [Eyal ch1]
- An idol caught in a humble setting, or a twist in the last seconds, gives people a reason to pass a clip on. [Kane ch5]
- A brand story told natively on each platform, in that platform's own language, travels further than the same ad pasted everywhere. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- A good story stirs the feeling that makes people do what you ask; no story, no sale. [Vaynerchuk R1]
- A story with conflict and mystery that resolves one question and opens the next keeps people coming back for the following part. [Eyal ch4]
- A brand is what people tell each other about it, not what the company says it is. [Schaefer ch7]
