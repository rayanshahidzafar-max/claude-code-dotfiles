# Contagious Content

This skill makes things worth passing on. It works on a single post as well as on a product, a launch or a whole campaign, and it produces the **contagion sheet** (`references/contagion-sheet.md`):
- a STEPPS score for the piece as it stands;
- the two or more principles it will lean on, and why;
- the remarkable thing, the trigger, the emotion and the visible signal;
- the story the brand rides inside;
- the changes to make before it ships.

For the opening line, use `viral-hooks`. For variations to test, use `audience-growth`. For the caption's flow, use `slippery-copy`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- What catches on isn't explained by how good it is, what it costs or how much it's advertised. Two equally good, equally free things can differ a hundredfold in popularity, and the gap is social transmission. [Berger, Intro]
- Word of mouth persuades better than ads, because friends tell it straight, and it targets better, because people pick the friend it suits. [Berger, Intro]
- Most word of mouth still happens face to face. Social platforms are tools for sharing, not a reason for anyone to share. [Berger, Intro]
- Spend less effort hunting influencers and more on the message. Some content travels whoever tells it, the way a great joke gets a laugh from a poor teller. [Berger, Intro]
- Virality is built, not born. A plain product can be made talkable once someone finds the right angle on it. [Berger, Intro]
- The six principles are social currency, triggers, emotion, public, practical value and stories. They work like toppings, not a recipe: one or two strong ones can carry a piece. [Berger, Intro]
- Learn from failures as well as hits. Looking only at viral winners teaches the wrong lessons, because plenty of funny or cute content goes nowhere. [Berger, Intro]
- The share is the metric that matters. A view or a like costs the viewer nothing; a share puts their name behind it. [Kane ch5]
- People share what helps their own standing or helps a friend, so ask what the sharer gets before asking what the brand gets. [Kane ch5]

## How to use this in practice

1. **Name what should spread:** the post, the product, the offer, the behaviour, the idea. [Berger, Epilogue]
2. **Score it against STEPPS** with the table below, 0 to 3 for each. Be honest about the zeros. → `references/contagion-sheet.md` [Berger, Epilogue]
3. **Choose at least two principles to strengthen.** Pick the ones the product can carry naturally rather than forcing all six. [Berger, Intro]
4. **Find the inner remarkability:** the fact, result or pattern-break that would make the sharer look sharp. → `references/social-currency.md` [Berger ch1]
5. **Find or grow a trigger:** a cue the audience meets often, close to the moment of use. → `references/triggers.md` [Berger ch2]
6. **Choose the emotion by arousal, not by mood.** Awe, amusement, excitement, anger and anxiety move people; sadness and contentment settle them. → `references/emotion.md` [Berger ch3]
7. **Make it visible.** Decide how use or support shows to others, during and after. → `references/public-and-value.md` [Berger ch4]
8. **Package the useful part** so it's short, specific and easy to forward to one named friend. → `references/public-and-value.md` [Berger ch5]
9. **Build the story** and check the brand can't be dropped from the retelling. → `references/stories.md` [Berger ch6]
10. **Write the sheet,** then hand the piece to `audience-growth` to test variations before spending on it. [Kane ch4]

## STEPPS at a glance

| Principle | The question | Strong when |
|---|---|---|
| Social currency | Does sharing it make the sharer look good? | it's remarkable, makes them an insider, or shows off an achievement |
| Triggers | What everyday cue brings it to mind? | the cue is frequent, specific and near the moment of use |
| Emotion | Does it fire a high-arousal feeling? | it brings awe, amusement, excitement, anger or anxiety, not quiet sadness |
| Public | Can others see people using or backing it? | use leaves a visible sign that lasts |
| Practical value | Does it help someone the sharer knows? | it saves money, time or trouble and fits one friend in particular |
| Stories | Is it carried inside a tale people want to tell? | the brand is the point of the story, not a detail |

- People gain standing from what they talk about, so make the product a way for them to look clever, kind or in the know. [Berger ch1]
- Whatever is on people's minds is what they mention. A frequent cue keeps a thing in conversation long after the first burst. [Berger ch2]
- Feelings drive action, so lead with the feeling and let the facts follow. [Berger ch3]
- People copy what they can see. A product nobody can see anyone using can't spread by imitation. [Berger ch4]
- Useful advice is passed on because people like helping each other, and it is the easiest principle to find in any product. [Berger ch5]
- People think in narratives. Information that rides inside a story travels further than information on its own. [Berger ch6]

## Where the books disagree

- **Paid push or pure organic?** Berger shows word of mouth beating ads and built from the message alone. Kane says hope is a poor plan and even shareable content needs paid fuel to start. Resolution used here: design for sharing first, then use small paid tests to find which version spreads, and spend only behind the versions people already share on their own.
- **Ask for the share or not?** Ellis's referral loops ask outright, with a reward. Kane and Berger warn that asking bluntly and paying people to share both kill the urge. Resolution: never just say "share this" or "tag a friend." Give a reason to involve a particular friend, and if there's a reward, make it part of the product's own value.
- **Interesting or frequent?** Remarkable things get talked about at once; frequently cued things keep getting mentioned. Resolution: a launch needs the remarkable thing; a product that must sell for years needs a trigger. Most plans need both, in that order.
- **Negative emotion?** Anger and anxiety spread well, but Vaynerchuk and Schaefer warn that brands which provoke lose trust. Resolution: use anger at a shared problem, never at a person or a customer, and never invent outrage.
- **Fads.** Riding a trend gives a fast trigger, but Sugarman lost money on fads that turned sour. Resolution: tie into a trend only when the link is honest and the product doesn't depend on the trend lasting.
