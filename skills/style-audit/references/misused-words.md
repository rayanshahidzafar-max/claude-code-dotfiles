# Commonly Misused Words and Expressions

A working subset of the book's list, paraphrased. The whole list rests on one principle: use each word in its exact sense, and cut the words that only pad. [S&W IV]

| Word or phrase | The problem | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| aggravate | means "make worse," not "annoy" | irritate, annoy (for people) |
| anticipate | not a synonym for simple expectation | expect |
| as good or better than | the comparison breaks | as good as or better than; or recast |
| as to whether | padding | whether |
| case | usually unnecessary ("in many cases") | recast: "many rooms…" |
| character, nature | usually filler ("remarks of an insulting nature") | insults |
| claim (verb) | means "lay claim to," not "say" | declare, maintain, charge |
| compare to / with | "to" likens things; "with" examines differences | choose by meaning |
| comprise | the whole comprises the parts | "consists of"; never "is comprised of" |
| contact (verb) | vague and self-important | call, write, meet |
| currently | redundant with a present-tense verb | usually delete |
| data | plural | "the data are" |
| different than | logic favors "differs from" | different from |
| disinterested | means impartial, not bored | uninterested (for bored) |
| due to | loose for "because of" | because of, owing to |
| effect / affect | effect (noun) = result; effect (verb) = bring about | check the sense |
| enormity | means monstrous wickedness | enormousness, size |
| fact | only for what can be verified, not judgments | claim, opinion, belief |
| facility | a vague stand-in for a real place | prison, school, hospital |
| factor, feature | hackneyed; add nothing | say what happened or what it did |
| farther / further | farther = distance; further = time, degree, quantity | choose by meaning |
| finalize, -ize coinages | pompous verbs made by tacking -ize on a noun | finish, complete; use an existing verb |
| fortuitous | means by chance, not fortunate | lucky |
| he is a man who | padding | "He…" |
| hopefully | as "I hope" it's ambiguous and grates on many ears | I hope; it is hoped |
| however (first word) | at the start it means "in whatever way" | move it inside: "At last, however, …" |
| imply / infer | the speaker implies; the listener infers | choose by who is doing it |
| importantly | usually a weak connective | "What's more," or recast |
| in regard to | never "in regards to" | as regards, about |
| in terms of | padding | recast: "The salary made the job unattractive" |
| insightful | an inflated "perceptive" | perceptive |
| interesting (as an intro) | announcing interest doesn't create it | just tell it |
| kind of / sort of | loose for "rather" | rather, somewhat, or cut |
| lay / lie | lay takes an object; lie doesn't | lie down; lay it down |
| less / fewer | less = quantity; fewer = number | fewer workers, less water |
| like / as | like goes before nouns; as before clauses | "as they did then" |
| literally | not for exaggeration | cut it, or use "almost" |
| meaningful | a bankrupt adjective | say what it meant |
| nauseous / nauseated | nauseous = sickening; nauseated = sickened | "I felt nauseated" |
| personally | usually unnecessary | delete |
| possess | puffed-up "have" or "own" | have, own |
| presently | ambiguous: "soon" or "now" | use it for "soon" only |
| state (verb) | not a stand-in for "say" | say; keep "state" for "express fully" |
| that / which | that restricts (tells which one); which adds a fact, with commas | "The key that opens…" / "The key, which is bent, …" |
| try and | takes the infinitive | try to |
| unique | no degrees: without equal | unique (never "very unique") |
| utilize | prefer the plain word | use |
| very | weakens more than it strengthens | a stronger word, or nothing |
| while | loose for "and," "but," "although" | a precise connective, or a semicolon |
| would (habitual) | unnecessary when "every day" already signals habit | simple past: "Each spring he visited…" |

## More from Strunk's 1918 list

The 1918 original sorts its list into three kinds of entry:
- plain errors;
- forms with defenders that are still best avoided;
- good words that keep pushing into places where they mean nothing.

The third kind stops being a problem once you aim at saying your own exact thought and refuse ready-made formulas. [S&W 1918 V]

When you catch one, don't swap in a synonym. Recast the sentence so it says the thing directly. [S&W 1918 V]

Entries the later edition dropped or shortened (paraphrased, with original examples): [S&W 1918 V]

| Word or phrase | The problem | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| all right | always two words; on the page, best kept for saying yes in conversation | all right |
| but (after doubt, help) | redundant: "I don't doubt but that she'll come" | I don't doubt she'll come; couldn't help noticing |
| but … but | a reservation stacked on a reservation | rearrange so one "but" does the work |
| can / may | can = is able; it isn't a stand-in for permission | may (for permission) |
| certainly | sprayed on every claim like "very"; a mannerism | cut it, or prove the claim |
| clever | overused; best kept for ingenuity in small things | ingenious, skilful, or say what was done |
| consider (as) | drop "as" when the verb means "judge" or "think" | "I consider her the best candidate" |
| dependable | a needless stand-in | reliable, trustworthy |
| divided into / composed of | a play is divided into acts; a poem is composed of stanzas | choose by meaning |
| don't / doesn't | "don't" is "do not"; "does not" is "doesn't" | "she doesn't know" |
| effect (in writing about art) | vague: "a striking effect," "subtle effects" | say what you saw or heard |
| etc. | only when the reader can supply the rest; never after "such as" or "for example" | finish the list, or cut "etc." |
| fix | colloquial for arrange, repair or prepare | arrange, mend; keep "fix" for "fasten" |
| have got | colloquial for "have" | have |
| line, along these lines | worn out | about, on, or name the subject |
| lose out, win out, meet up with, head up | the particle adds nothing; keep it only where it changes the meaning (find out, cheer up) | lose, win, meet, head |
| most (for almost) | "most everyone" | almost everyone |
| nature (as a vague subject) | "a lover of nature": scenery, farming, weather, or wildlife? | name which |
| oftentimes | archaic | often |
| one of the most | a threadbare way to open an essay or paragraph | open with the specific claim |
| possessive before an -ing noun | "the chance of the board approving" blurs what the chance is of | the board's approving; or recast: "the chance that the board will approve" |
| people / public | the people give political support; the public gives artistic or commercial patronage | choose by meaning |
| phase | a stage of change (phases of the moon), not an aspect or topic | aspect, point, question |
| respective, respectively | usually removable | cut it |
| so (as intensifier) | "so tired," "so good" | a stronger word, or say how much |
| split infinitive | the 1918 text calls it a fault; the later edition lets a split stand when it reads better | flag only splits that sound clumsy |
| student body | means only "students" | students |
| system | often padding: "the dormitory system" | dormitories |
| thanking you in advance | sounds as if you won't bother to thank later | ask politely now; thank afterward |
| viewpoint | write "point of view," and don't use it for "opinion" | point of view; opinion |
| whom (before "he said") | "the man whom she thought was guilty": the pronoun is the subject of "was" | who |
| worth while | vague approval; strictly it applies to actions | worth reading, worth the trip |
