# White's Twenty-One Reminders

White's list from his chapter "An Approach to Style," paraphrased. Cite them in the audit report as V1–V21.

1. **Place yourself in the background.** Point the reader at what the writing says, not at the writer's mood or cleverness. [S&W V.1]
2. **Write naturally.** Use the words that come readily. Imitation while learning is inevitable, but never imitate on purpose. And don't assume what comes naturally is automatically good. [S&W V.2]
3. **Work from a suitable design.** Even the most spontaneous-seeming writing has a plan underneath it. [S&W V.3]
4. **Write with nouns and verbs.** Adjectives and adverbs can't rescue a weak or wrong noun. [S&W V.4]
5. **Revise and rewrite.** Rewriting belongs to the job, not after it. Big rearrangements are normal; keep the old version to compare. [S&W V.5]
6. **Don't overwrite.** Rich, ornate prose is hard to digest. The keyboard's easy flow tempts you on, so reread later and cut the excess. [S&W V.6]
7. **Don't overstate.** One exaggeration makes the reader doubt everything else. A careless superlative can sink the very thing you're praising. [S&W V.7]
8. **Avoid qualifiers.** "Rather," "very," "little," "pretty": they drain the words around them. [S&W V.8]
9. **Don't affect a breezy manner.** A chatty, self-regarding style usually has nothing to say. Get straight to the news. [S&W V.9]
10. **Use orthodox spelling.** Save cute spellings for deliberate effect. [S&W V.10]
11. **Don't explain too much.** It's seldom wise to tell all. Skip the adverb after "he said"; let the conversation show the manner. Don't load dialogue tags with explaining verbs. [S&W V.11]
12. **Don't build awkward adverbs.** "Tiredly," "overly," "thusly": words nobody says aloud rarely belong on the page. [S&W V.12]
13. **Make sure the reader knows who is speaking.** In long exchanges, identify speakers often enough. Put each tag where the speaker would naturally pause; test it by reading aloud. [S&W V.13]
14. **Avoid fancy words.** Don't spend an expensive word where a cheap one will do. Plain, short, old words are usually livelier than long Latinate ones. In the end it's a matter of ear. [S&W V.14]
15. **Use dialect only if your ear is good.** Be consistent, and use the fewest departures from standard spelling that still suggest the voice. [S&W V.15]
16. **Be clear.** Check that you actually said what you meant; often you haven't. When a sentence bogs down, start fresh, often by splitting it in two. [S&W V.16]
17. **Don't inject opinion without a reason.** Unwanted opinion reads as ego. [S&W V.17]
18. **Use figures of speech sparingly.** A rapid string of similes distracts. Never mix metaphors. [S&W V.18]
19. **Don't take shortcuts at the cost of clarity.** Spell out initials and abbreviations the first time. The long way round is often the shortest way home. [S&W V.19]
20. **Avoid foreign phrases** used only to impress. [S&W V.20]
21. **Prefer the standard to the offbeat.** Join the language quietly: avoid jargon, business coinages and critics' vogue words. Novelty can be a cover for laziness. [S&W V.21]

## White's closing ideas

- Your style is you. In the end, who you are shapes your writing more than what you know. [S&W V]
- The reader is often in difficulty. Be patient with that, but never condescend. [S&W V]
