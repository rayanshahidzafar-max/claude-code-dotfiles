# The Reader Path

The reader path maps how readers move through everything set in a world: where they can start, which way they go, and what pulls them along. Keep it at `stories/series/<series-slug>/reader-path.md`.

```
READING ORDER:        story order, and publication order where it differs
ENTRY POINTS:         every book a newcomer can safely start with
THE WORLD'S BOOKS:    series, spin-offs, companions, prequels, each placed on the timeline
HOOKS:                each book's last page → the book it sells
BONUS & GIVEAWAYS:    prequel stories, bonus epilogues, short stories, crossovers
BUNDLES:              which books, and when
```

## Book one is the engine

- Every reader of book five came through book one, so put your effort, polish and promotion there. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Make book one the best book you can, even if you'll give it away. A free book that disappoints sends nobody on to book two. [Tomlinson ch18]

## More stories, more ways in

- Readers rarely complain about getting more of a world they love. [Rosett, Marketing]
- A prequel can be a gift. Mention a past event in book one without telling it, write the story of that event, offer it to readers (as a newsletter gift, say), and update book one's back matter to point to it. [Rosett, Marketing]
- A bonus epilogue carries on past the ending (the wedding, the baby), and romance readers especially love one. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Short stories can spotlight secondary characters, fill gaps and add background. They work in anthologies, bundles, collections and audio. [Rosett, Marketing]
- A giveaway story works best as a complete story, not a sample chapter, written in the main books' style and at their quality. Between books, giveaways also keep readers warm through long gaps. [Scheuerer ch19]
- Give every bonus piece the series' full quality. Free stories are ambassadors, and readers judge the books by them. [Tomlinson ch21]

## Bundles and release timing

- Bundles suit binge readers. Bundle in threes or the whole series, or make a sampler of first books. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Release the books one at a time first and bundle them later, adding bonus content so existing readers have a reason to buy the bundle too. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Price the series as a path: a free or discounted first book, short free windows, deep temporary discounts, later books at full price. Every free copy trades some mismatched readers, and their reviews, for reach. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Rotating discounts on later books only work with a deep backlist, and run too often they teach readers to wait for the sale. [Rosett, Marketing]
- Rapid release (books thirty to ninety days apart) keeps a series visible and shows readers you'll finish it. It also sets expectations of speed you may not keep, so batch-write first if you plan to do it. [Tomlinson ch22]
- Batch writing (drafting several books before releasing any) lets you plant clues back into earlier books and fix continuity, at the cost of time before any income. Books written together also tend to sit closer together in story time, which changes the series' feel. [Rosett, Batch Writing]

Offers, prices and funnels belong to the persuasion skills. This file decides where each book sits on the path, not how it's sold.

## Reading-order rules

- Every book must work for a reader who starts there. Don't spoil earlier books' big surprises, and use subtle reminders that mean something only to readers who came in order. [Tomlinson ch23]
- Tell readers how long the series will be: a trilogy, a quartet, or an honest "six to nine books, depending on the story". Readers are patient with writers who tell them. [Scheuerer ch27]
- Publish a clear reading order in the back matter, on the retailer page and on your website, especially when prequels and spin-offs make story order differ from publication order. [Scheuerer ch23]
- Keep covers, typography and titling consistent within a series and distinct between series, so readers can see at a glance which books belong together. [Tomlinson ch6]
