# Gathering

## The first members

- Trust comes first: newcomers ask whether they trust this place. [Schaefer ch8]
- The first members set the tone, like session musicians on a record; choose them. [Schaefer ch8]
- Early members come from existing connections. [Schaefer ch8]
- One founder gathered her first members, with no ad budget, by sharing her book decisions (titles, covers) in groups she already belonged to. The research became a movement. [Schaefer ch4]
- Host where the community already is, somewhere with a collective voice. Comments under posts aren't a community. [Schaefer ch4]
- Start with a few friends and existing networks. [Schaefer ch5]
- Go where your audience already exists. [Kane ch6]
- Find existing communities where the target users gather and show up there. [Ellis ch2]

## Why people join

- They join for personal connection, status or self-improvement, entertainment, and information. [Schaefer ch8]
- Follow fans where they take you, even off your plan, as brands did when gamers moved to live-streaming. [Schaefer ch8]
- Reinforce online ties with live events. [Schaefer ch8]
- Online communities often go on to meet in person. [Schaefer ch1]
- Audio can lower the barrier to showing up compared with video. [Schaefer ch1]
- Kindness as a core value, with clear guidelines, builds trust. [Schaefer ch1]

## Growing it

- Organic growth is a myth; market the community. [Schaefer ch8]
- Show its work outside so non-members feel they're missing out. [Schaefer ch8]
- Promote it everywhere: email signature, website, talks. [Schaefer ch8]
- Personal invitations work better than broadcasts. [Schaefer ch8]
- A newsletter grew into a lunch club, turning an audience into a community. [Schaefer ch8]
- Awards, a podcast spotlighting members and delegated leadership grow it. [Schaefer ch8]
- The best growth comes through member referrals. [Schaefer ch4]
- When launch energy fades, re-energise with new value. [Schaefer ch4]
- Monetise later: a free group first, then offers, then guided programmes that meet the real need. [Schaefer ch4]
- Members of a busy community want decision support more than a library. [Schaefer ch4]
- A referral reward should pay in core value, never cash that attracts the wrong people. [Ellis ch5]
- The invitee should land somewhere that shows the inviter's name and a reason to stay. [Ellis ch5]
