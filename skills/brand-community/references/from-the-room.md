# What the Rest of the Room Adds

## Why members return

- People come back through habits: a trigger, an easy action, a variable reward and an investment. [Eyal, Intro]
- Loneliness and boredom are internal triggers; a community can be the answer people reach for. [Eyal ch2]
- The tribe reward is social: being seen, replied to and accepted. [Eyal ch4]
- People learn from peers slightly ahead of them, which makes a community of practice rewarding. [Eyal ch4]
- Infinite variety comes from other people, so a living community doesn't wear out the way a finished series does. [Eyal ch4]
- What members put in (posts, reputation, friends) is stored value that makes leaving costly. [Eyal ch5]
- Ask for investment after a reward, not before. [Eyal ch5]
- Encouragement from other people is a relationship trigger that brings members back. [Eyal ch7]
- A habit built for people's benefit is fair; one that exploits them isn't. [Eyal ch6]

## Status and visibility

- Status and insider feelings make people talk. [Berger ch1]
- Name something after a member or put them on stage, and they'll tell everyone. [Berger ch1]
- Make belonging visible with residue that lasts: a sticker, a wristband, a badge. [Berger ch4]
- People copy what they see others do. [Berger ch4]
- Good stories carry the brand when it's central to the tale. [Berger ch6]

## The conversation

- Effort and charm in conversation beat bigger budgets. [Vaynerchuk R9]
- Talk about what members are talking about; switch when they switch. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Brand voice should feel like a real person. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Small brands win with humanity and a little self-deprecation. [Vaynerchuk R4]
- Fans who talk to each other on a site spend more time there. [Kane, Intro]
- Treat fans as friends and earn their trust. [Kane ch12]
- Feature fans in the content. [Kane ch9]
- Let the community act as editors. [Kane ch4]

## Retention and value

- A small lift in retention is worth a lot of profit. [Ellis ch7]
- Discounts that bring in bargain hunters fill a community with people who leave. [Ellis ch7]
- Introduce features or programmes gradually, once members have mastered the basics. [Ellis ch7]
- Learn why members left before trying to bring them back, and don't nag. [Ellis ch7]
- Tease what's coming soon. [Ellis ch7]
- Rewards that fit the core value: status, access, power, stuff. [Ellis ch6]

## Selling to members

- Wanting to belong drives purchases: show that people like the reader use it. [Sugarman ch19]
- Human relationships in copy, like a byline and a real voice, build trust. [Sugarman ch19]
- Address a single reader, not a crowd. [Sugarman ch15]
- Small-company honesty letters build loyalty. [Sugarman ch15]
