# Brand Community

This skill turns followers into people who belong. It produces the **community plan** (`references/community-plan.md`):
- where the people sit on the follower-audience-community continuum;
- the purpose, in the member's terms;
- where to gather and who the first members are;
- the repeatable activities, questions, polls and rituals;
- the ladder of roles and the status that fuels it;
- the ground rules;
- what to measure.

For the brand story with the customer as hero, use `storybrand`. For what gets shared outside, use `contagious-content`. For the jabs that grow the audience first, use `platform-native`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- About two-thirds of marketing now happens without the brand: in reviews, posts and word of mouth. [Schaefer, Intro]
- Helping someone belong is the highest thing marketing can achieve. [Schaefer, Intro]
- Most brand communities fail because they're a place to sell or a self-service help desk, not a place to help. [Schaefer, Intro]
- Brands are systems of meaning, and community may be the last great marketing strategy. [Schaefer, Intro]
- A brand is the expectation people form from their past experiences with it. [Schaefer ch2]
- The emotional continuum runs from social follower (a weak link), to audience (opted-in subscribers), to community (members connected to each other). [Schaefer ch2]
- A community needs connection between members, a shared purpose and continued relevance. [Schaefer ch2]
- A conference isn't a community; what members do between gatherings is. [Schaefer ch2]
- Loneliness is widespread, and belonging changes people's lives. [Schaefer ch1]
- In an audience you help people; in a community, people help each other. [Schaefer ch8]
- Personal contact with individual fans turns them into advocates who build with you. [Kane, Intro]

## How to use this in practice

1. **Place the brand on the continuum:** followers, audience or community. [Schaefer ch2]
2. **Write the purpose** as a customer-centred dream, not a sales goal. → `references/purpose-and-culture.md` [Schaefer ch5]
3. **Pick where to gather:** where the people already are, with a collective voice. → `references/gathering.md` [Schaefer ch4]
4. **Choose the first members** like session musicians; they set the tone. → `references/gathering.md` [Schaefer ch8]
5. **Design repeatable activities** that are purposeful and participatory: questions, polls, challenges, live shows, rituals. → `references/activities.md` [Schaefer ch7]
6. **Build the ladder:** welcomers, connectors, rule-keepers, leaders, and grant status. → `references/leadership.md` [Schaefer ch4]
7. **Set ground rules early** and remove toxicity. → `references/leadership.md` [Schaefer, Conclusion]
8. **Help members spread it outside** and run the sticker test. → `references/measurement.md` [Schaefer ch10]
9. **Measure as brand marketing,** not direct response. → `references/measurement.md` [Schaefer ch10]
10. **Fill in the plan,** start before it's perfect, and let members improve it. → `references/community-plan.md` [Schaefer ch7]

## Where the books disagree

- **Grow big or stay close?** Kane chases millions of followers; Schaefer says bigger dilutes connection. Resolution: grow the audience wide, and the community narrow and deep.
- **Measure it or trust it?** Ellis wants every lever measured; Schaefer says forcing direct attribution on community kills it. Resolution: track engagement rate, referrals and outside mentions as leading signs, and judge sales over quarters, not posts.
- **Own platform or theirs?** Vaynerchuk wants owned media; Schaefer says don't build your own house, go where members gather. Resolution: gather where they are, and keep an owned line (newsletter) beside it.
- **Gamify?** Eyal and Ellis use status rewards; Schaefer warns about members who join for money. Resolution: reward status and contribution, never cash speculation.
