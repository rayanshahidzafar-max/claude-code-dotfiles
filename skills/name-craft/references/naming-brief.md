# The Naming Brief

## Why a brief comes first

- Creativity needs ingredients. Without a brief, names drift off strategy, because the namer is missing the facts that would have ruled them in or out. [Watkins ch4]
- The brief defines what the thing is and what the name should do, and it guards against a wrong choice later. [Watkins ch4]
- Get every decision maker to sign off on the brief before brainstorming starts. Agreeing on the brief is how you later agree on a name. [Watkins ch4]
- Research the people before writing about them. Read the questions they ask, the reviews they write and the words they use, especially the balanced two-to-four-star reviews. [Edwards S12]
- Describe the audience by what goes on in their heads (problems, questions, roadblocks, hoped-for results), not only by age and income. [Edwards S8]
- Listen for the exact verbs the audience uses for what they want to do. Small differences in wording matter, and their word is the one that lands. [Edwards S22]

## How to fill the hard fields

- **Consumer insights** are about behaviour and circumstances, not preferences. Ask not what tea drinkers like about tea, but when they reach for a cup: after the commute, with a book, before bed. [Watkins ch4]
- **Desired experiences** are the thoughts you want people to have when they meet the thing: "this will be fun", "I can trust this", "my friends will love this". The best names evoke one of them. [Watkins ch4]
- **Personality** is five to twelve adjectives. It is far easier to write if you picture the brand as a person. [Watkins ch4]
- **Competitors** go in the brief so you know what you're up against and can steer clear of names that would cause trademark trouble. [Watkins ch4]
- **Words and themes to avoid** save hours. A client once banned "any sound a bird makes" because a famous brand had worn that pattern out. [Watkins ch2]
- **Style likes and dislikes:** five names you admire and five you don't, each with a reason. The reasons reveal the taste better than the lists do. [Watkins ch4]
- **The acid test** is a sentence with a blank where the name goes. Every candidate must read well in it. [Watkins ch4]

## Template

```
NAMING BRIEF — <project>

GOAL:                 <what you are naming, and what the name must do>
IN A NUTSHELL:        <the thing in 140 characters>
POSITIONING:          <for whom, against what, why it wins>
AUDIENCE:             <who; what they want; the words they use>
CONSUMER INSIGHTS:    <behaviours and circumstances, not preferences>
DESIRED EXPERIENCES:  <what they will think or feel: 3–6 short quotes>
PERSONALITY:          <5–12 adjectives>
COMPETITORS:          <names to steer clear of>
WORDS TO EXPLORE:     <candidate words and themes>
THEMES TO AVOID:      <and why>
WORDS TO AVOID:       <and why>
STYLE LIKES:          <5 names + why>
STYLE DISLIKES:       <5 names + why>
FUTURE:               <products, places or audiences the name must stretch to>
DOMAIN MODIFIERS:     <get/try/my/the ___ ; ___ co/app/store/hq>
ACID TEST:            "____ ____ <does what> for <whom> so they can <result>."
ALSO GOOD TO KNOW:    <places to go for inspiration; constraints>
```

For a story name, swap the business fields for the story's: the world, the culture that coined the name, who says it and how often, what the reader must feel on first meeting it, and which names already exist nearby.
