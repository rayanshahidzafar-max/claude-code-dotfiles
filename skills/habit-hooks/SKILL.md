# Habit Hooks

This skill gets people to come back. It works on a product, an app, a newsletter, a channel or a content series, and it produces the **hook map** (`references/hook-map.md`):
- the habit you want, and how often it should happen;
- the internal trigger (the feeling) and the external triggers timed to it;
- the simplest first action;
- the reward, its type, and what keeps it variable;
- the investment that loads the next trigger;
- the re-hooks and open loops that carry people from one piece to the next;
- a manipulation-matrix check.

For what makes a piece shareable, use `contagious-content`. For the opening line of each piece, use `viral-hooks`. For the community that keeps people around, use `brand-community`.

**Important:** never quote or reproduce text from the source books. Paraphrase the ideas, use your own examples, and apply them to the person's work.

## The core ideas

- A habit is something people do almost automatically, without deciding to. The product or creator that comes to mind first, without being called, wins. [Eyal, Intro]
- The Hook has four phases: a trigger, an action, a variable reward and an investment. [Eyal, Intro]
- Each pass through the Hook makes the next pass more likely, until people return on their own, without ads or reminders. [Eyal, Intro]
- Habits pay: more lifetime value, more room on price, faster growth because heavy users spread the word more often, and a moat against rivals. [Eyal ch1]
- A new thing has to be far better than the old habit, on the order of nine times, because people overvalue what they already do. [Eyal ch1]
- Frequency and perceived usefulness together make the habit zone. Something used rarely needs very high value to become a habit. [Eyal ch1]
- Habit products start as vitamins (nice to have) and become painkillers (missed when gone). They scratch an itch more than they cure a pain. [Eyal ch1]
- A habit is not an addiction. Building one is fair only when it helps the person who forms it. [Eyal ch6]
- Retention compounds: a small lift in the share of people who stay is worth more than a large lift in sign-ups who leave. [Ellis ch7]
- Content habits work the same way: a creator who posts daily small pieces becomes part of the routine, while one big campaign is forgotten. [Vaynerchuk R1]

## How to use this in practice

1. **Name the habit** and how often it should realistically happen (daily, weekly). If it can't happen often, it needs very high value. [Eyal ch1]
2. **Find the internal trigger:** the feeling (boredom, loneliness, confusion, fear of missing out) that comes just before the behaviour. Ask why five times. → `references/triggers.md` [Eyal ch2]
3. **Write the trigger sentence:** "Every time the user feels ___, they ___." [Eyal ch2]
4. **Choose external triggers** and time them close to when the feeling fires; prefer triggers people opt into. → `references/triggers.md` [Eyal ch2]
5. **Make the first action easy.** Find which of time, money, effort, thinking, social deviance or routine is scarcest, and remove it. → `references/action.md` [Eyal ch3]
6. **Pick the reward:** tribe, hunt or self. Make sure it fits why people came, and keep something unpredictable in it. → `references/variable-reward.md` [Eyal ch4]
7. **Ask for an investment after the reward:** stored work such as posts, settings, connections, standing or know-how, staged from small to big. → `references/investment.md` [Eyal ch5]
8. **Load the next trigger** inside the investment so the next visit starts itself. → `references/investment.md` [Eyal ch5]
9. **For a series, build the open loop:** each piece resolves one question and opens the next. → `references/open-loops.md` [Eyal ch4]
10. **Run the manipulation matrix** and the habit test, then fill in the map. → `references/ethics-and-testing.md`, `references/hook-map.md` [Eyal ch6]

## The Hook at a glance

| Phase | The question | Strong when |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | What prompts the visit? | an owned cue lands just as the feeling fires |
| Action | What is the simplest thing they do expecting a reward? | one tap, one scroll, one click |
| Variable reward | What do they get, and what's new each time? | it fits the itch and isn't fully predictable |
| Investment | What do they put in that makes next time better? | it stores value and loads the next trigger |

- External triggers tell people what to do next; internal triggers come from inside, as a feeling or a routine. [Eyal ch2]
- The behaviour has to take less effort than deliberating about it. The more effort a behaviour takes, the less likely it happens. [Eyal ch3]
- The brain lights up in anticipation of a reward more than on receiving it, and predictability kills interest. [Eyal ch4]
- People value what they put work into, so a small effort now makes the product more valuable later. [Eyal ch5]

## Where the books disagree

- **Habit or virality first?** Berger and Kane chase the share; Eyal says spreading is faster once people use the product often. Resolution: build the return loop for the core audience first, then push sharing; a viral spike on a product nobody returns to is a leaky bucket.
- **Notifications and reminders.** Eyal values owned triggers; Ellis warns against nagging and asking for opt-in before explaining the value. Resolution: ask for permission after the first reward, and only send triggers that point at real value.
- **Cliffhangers.** Open loops keep people coming back, but Kane warns that a hook that doesn't match the content kills trust. Resolution: every piece must pay off its own promise; the open loop is extra, never a substitute for the payoff.
- **Gamification.** Points, streaks and badges work in Eyal and Ellis only when they serve why people came. Resolution: no points for their own sake; reward progress toward the person's own goal.
