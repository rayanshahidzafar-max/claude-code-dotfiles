# Action

## Easier than thinking

- The action is the easiest thing a person does while expecting a payoff: a scroll, a tap, a search, a click on play. [Eyal ch3]
- For a behaviour to happen, three things must be present at once: motivation, ability and a trigger. [Eyal ch3]
- Without enough motivation or ability, the trigger is ignored or annoys. [Eyal ch3]
- Three pairs drive people: chasing pleasure or escaping pain, chasing hope or escaping fear, chasing belonging or escaping rejection. [Eyal ch3]
- Making the action easier usually beats raising motivation, because motivation is costly to raise and people ignore instructions. Start with ability. [Eyal ch3]
- Remove steps. Map every step between intent and reward and cut the ones that aren't needed. [Eyal ch3]

## Six kinds of friction

- Time: how long it takes. [Eyal ch3]
- Money: what it costs. [Eyal ch3]
- Physical effort: how much work it is. [Eyal ch3]
- Brain cycles: how hard it is to understand. [Eyal ch3]
- Social deviance: how far it is from what others do. [Eyal ch3]
- Non-routine: how different it is from the person's usual day. [Eyal ch3]
- Find the scarcest of the six for this person in this moment, and design around that one. [Eyal ch3]
- A homepage with two clear calls to action and nothing else gets more people moving than a busy one. [Eyal ch3]
- Growth is desire minus friction. Cut friction at the steps where people drop off, and test each cut. [Ellis ch6]
- Let people try before they sign up where you can, so they meet the value before the form. [Ellis ch6]
- One call to action per post. Two questions in a post is one too many. [Vaynerchuk R3]
- A simple offer beats a complex one, and one choice can outsell many. [Sugarman ch19]

## Shortcuts the mind takes

- Scarcity: something seems more valuable when there is little of it, and abundance after scarcity lowers its value. [Eyal ch3]
- Framing: the setting changes how good something seems. The same music or wine is judged differently depending on where and how it's offered. [Eyal ch3]
- Anchoring: a reference price makes a deal look better. [Eyal ch3]
- Endowed progress: people finish more often when they start with some progress already given, like a loyalty card with two stamps already in it. [Eyal ch3]
- Show progress bars and checkmarks; a half-finished set pulls people to complete it. [Eyal ch3]
- A small first commitment makes the next, bigger one more likely. [Sugarman ch19]
