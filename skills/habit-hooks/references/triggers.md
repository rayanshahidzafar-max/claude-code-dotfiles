# Triggers

## External triggers

- An external trigger tells people their next step: a button, a link, an email, a notification, an icon. [Eyal ch2]
- The fewer choices a trigger offers, the less people hesitate. One clear next step beats a menu. [Eyal ch2]
- Paid triggers (ads, search) work for getting people in but are too costly to bring the same people back again and again. [Eyal ch2]
- Earned triggers (press, a viral moment, a store feature) are free but short-lived; attention moves on. [Eyal ch2]
- Relationship triggers come from people telling each other, through word of mouth or invitations. Tricks that spam friends backfire. [Eyal ch2]
- Owned triggers are the ones people opt into: an app on the home screen, a newsletter sign-up, notification settings. They bring people back repeatedly. [Eyal ch2]
- The goal of every external trigger is to move people through the Hook often enough that they no longer need it. [Eyal ch2]
- A subscriber who opts in is an owned trigger; a follower on someone else's platform is rented. Build a direct line such as a newsletter. [Schaefer ch7]
- Data shows when the audience wants to hear from you. Post when they're looking, not when it suits the team. [Vaynerchuk R1]

## Internal triggers

- Internal triggers are feelings and routines that people come to associate with the product. Once linked, the feeling alone prompts the action. [Eyal ch2]
- Negative feelings make the strongest triggers: boredom, loneliness, frustration, confusion, indecision and fear of missing out. [Eyal ch2]
- Linking a feeling to a product takes weeks or months of repeated use; it doesn't happen on the first visit. [Eyal ch2]
- Describe the user's pain in emotional terms, not in features. Nobody wants email; they don't want to feel out of the loop. [Eyal ch2]
- What people say they want and what they do can differ. Watch what they do. [Eyal ch2]
- Write a short user story: who they are, where they are, what they feel just before they reach for the product. [Eyal ch2]
- Ask why the behaviour happens, and why that matters, about five times, until you reach a feeling. [Eyal ch2]
- Fill in the sentence: "Every time the user feels ___, they ___." If you can't, you haven't found the trigger. [Eyal ch2]
- Time external triggers to arrive when the internal trigger fires, so the cue lands with the itch. [Eyal ch2]
- A cue the audience meets every day keeps a thing talked about long after the launch. [Berger ch2]
- Find the moments in the audience's day when the feeling rises, and pair the product with one of them repeatedly. [Berger ch2]

## Triggers for content

- For a creator, the internal trigger is often a mood: bored on the commute, stuck on a problem, wanting a laugh. Make the content the answer to that mood. [Eyal ch2]
- A fixed time and format (every Tuesday, every morning) teaches the audience when to come looking. [Eyal ch8]
- Jab every day: small daily pieces keep the creator in the routine. [Vaynerchuk R2]
- Consistency on one topic with a clear point of view is what keeps viewers coming back to a channel. [Kane ch9]
- An email subject line works like the envelope: short, personal and worth opening. [Sugarman ch36]
